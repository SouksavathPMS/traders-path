## 5.1 [[5.1 What Liquidity Really Is|What Liquidity Really Is]]

You are short EUR/USD. Your stop is just above the equal highs. Is your stop part of BSL or SSL?
?
BSL. A short's stop is a **buy** order, and it rests above the highs.

Why are equal highs a bigger pool than a single high?
?
They're more obvious. More traders see them as resistance, short there with stops just above, and breakout traders place buy entries just above, so more orders pile up.

Why does a fund that wants to sell a lot like price to rise into buy stops first?
?
Because the buy stops become market buy orders. The fund can sell its large size into that buying at a high price, without pushing price down against itself.

Which is external liquidity: the equal highs at the top of the range, or an FVG in the middle of the range?
?
The equal highs (beyond the range). The FVG inside the range is internal liquidity.

In one sentence, what is liquidity and where does it pool?
?
Liquidity is the resting orders available to trade against; it pools just beyond obvious highs and lows (equal highs/lows, PDH/PDL, session extremes, round numbers), where stops and breakout entries cluster.

200 longs each have a 0.2-lot stop under the same low. How many lots of market sells is that, and in which pool?
?
200 × 0.2 = 40 lots of sells, part of the SSL below the low.


## 5.2 [[5.2 Liquidity Sweeps & Stop Hunts|Liquidity Sweeps & Stop Hunts]]

In the walkthrough, what would have cancelled the trade idea after candle 2?
?
A candle **closing** back below the equal lows (below 1.0781) and staying there: that would be acceptance below, a run rather than a sweep.

Why is the stop at 1.0765 and not at 1.0781?
?
The sweep low (1.0768) is where the idea is proven wrong. 1.0781 is inside the noise of the sweep itself, and a second poke is common. The stop goes beyond the sweep extreme plus a buffer.

After the candle has closed, how do you tell a sweep from a real breakout?
?
Look at the close. Sweep: a wick beyond the level but the **close is back inside**, followed by displacement the other way. Real breakout: the **close is beyond** the level and the next candles hold there (a retest holds, BOS in the same direction).

Price closes above the equal highs, pulls back, and the retest holds as support. Sweep or run?
?
A run. The close beyond plus a retest that holds is acceptance (role reversal, 2.4).

The daily trend is up. You see a sweep of equal **highs** during London. Should it carry as much weight as a sweep of equal **lows**?
?
Less. In a daily uptrend, sweeps of SSL (below lows) during pullbacks fit the HTF bias. A sweep of the highs against the trend more often turns into a run.

List the five things that must happen, in order, before you short a sweep of equal highs.
?
1) An obvious pool (equal highs, PDH). 2) A wick above it with a close back inside. 3) Displacement down. 4) A close below the swing that launched the sweep (CHoCH/MSS). 5) A retrace entry with the stop above the sweep high + buffer and R:R ≥ 2 to the opposite pool.

Entry 1.0798, stop 1.0765, target 1.0880. What is the R:R?
?
Risk 33 pips, reward 82 pips → 82 ÷ 33 ≈ 2.5R.


## 5.3 [[5.3 Dealing Range- Premium & Discount|Dealing Range: Premium & Discount]]

A leg runs from 50.00 to 60.00. Where is equilibrium, and is 57.00 premium or discount?
?
Equilibrium = 55.00. 57.00 is above it, so it's premium (expensive for buying).

A down-leg runs from **1.2700** to **1.2500**. For a short, where is the OTE (62–79% retracement)?
?
Range 200 pips. 1.2500 + 0.62 × 200 pips = 1.2624; 1.2500 + 0.79 × 200 pips = 1.2658 → OTE 1.2624–1.2658 (above equilibrium 1.2600, i.e. in premium).

Price is inside the OTE but there's no FVG, order block or swept level there. Do you buy?
?
No. The OTE is a location, not a reason. You still need a PD array and an LTF trigger.

On the 15-minute chart price is in discount, but on the daily chart it's in the top 10% of the range. Is this an A-grade long?
?
No. It's cheap inside an expensive area. The best longs are in discount on both the HTF and the LTF range.

Why does buying in discount improve R:R even when the stop and target don't change?
?
Because the entry is closer to the stop (less risk) and further from the target (more reward). The same idea becomes a much better bet.

Up-leg 2,000 → 2,100. Give equilibrium and the OTE band for longs.
?
Equilibrium 2,050. OTE = 2,100 − 0.62 × 100 = 2,038 to 2,100 − 0.79 × 100 = 2,021 → 2,021–2,038.


## 5.4 [[5.4 Fair Value Gaps (FVG)|Fair Value Gaps (FVG)]]

Candle 1 low **50.00**, candle 3 high **49.70**, with a big red candle in between. What is it, how big, and where is the CE?
?
A bearish FVG (a SIBI): 50.00 − 49.70 = 0.30; CE = 49.85. It's invalid if a candle closes above 50.00.

Candle 1 high 100.20, candle 3 low 100.15. Is there an FVG?
?
No. Candle 3's low is below candle 1's high, so the candles overlap: no gap.

Why are FVGs counted as internal liquidity?
?
They sit inside the current range, and they hold unfilled orders from traders who missed the fast move. Price is often drawn back into them before going on to the next external pool.

Same trade, but you choose the more aggressive entry at the top of the gap (100.60). What's the new risk per unit and size?
?
Risk = 100.60 − 99.90 = 0.70 → size = 100 ÷ 0.70 = 142.9 → 142 units. Better fill chance, smaller size.

Define a bullish FVG in one sentence, using the three candles.
?
Three candles where the middle one is a big up candle and candle 3's low is above candle 1's high; the gap between them is the FVG.

What exactly makes an FVG invalid?
?
A candle **closing** through the far side of the gap (below candle 1's high for a bullish FVG). A wick through that closes back inside does not invalidate it.


## 5.5 [[5.5 Order Blocks & Breakers|Order Blocks & Breakers]]

An up-move starts from the last red candle, but it's slow, overlapping and doesn't break any swing high. Is that last red candle an order block?
?
No. Without displacement and a BOS, nothing proves a large participant was there. Every move has a "last opposite candle".

Why is the first return to an OB stronger than the second?
?
The first return fills the remaining orders (mitigation). After that there are no unfilled orders left waiting there.

A bearish OB at 55.0–55.6 is closed through to the upside at 56.4. When price returns to 55.6, what is it likely to act as, and why?
?
Support (a bullish breaker). Shorts who sold at the OB are trapped; when price returns they buy back at break-even, and new buyers join.

Give the four conditions that make a "last opposite candle" a real order block.
?
Displacement away from it, a break of structure (BOS/MSS), ideally an FVG left behind, and a location in discount (bullish) or premium (bearish). Fresh and in the HTF direction are bonuses.

What turns a bullish OB into a bearish breaker?
?
A candle **closing** below the OB. Traders who bought there are trapped and sell at break-even when price returns, so the old support becomes resistance.


## 5.6 [[5.6 Time- Sessions, Killzones & Quarterly Theory|Time: Sessions, Killzones & Quarterly Theory]]

It's December. At what time UTC+7 does the NY AM killzone start?
?
07:00 NY + 12 h = 19:00 UTC+7 (US winter).

Why do Thai and Lao traders see the killzones move, even though Thailand and Laos never change their clocks?
?
Because New York and London change theirs (daylight saving). The windows are fixed in NY time, so they move in UTC+7.

Your daily bias is bearish. Where do you prefer to look for shorts: above or below the daily open?
?
Above it. On a bearish day the Judas swing often goes up first (manipulation), so the best short prices tend to be above the open.

It's August and 20:00 in Bangkok. Which quarter of the ICT day is it?
?
Q3 (NY AM): 17:00–23:00 UTC+7 in US summer.

What should you do with quarterly theory before trading it?
?
Test it on your own data (Phase 9). It's the least proven part of ICT; treat it as a hypothesis, not a rule.

Fill in UTC+7 for the London killzone and the NY AM killzone, in US summer and in US winter.
?
Summer: London 13:00–16:00, NY AM 18:00–21:00. Winter: London 14:00–17:00, NY AM 19:00–22:00.

Which part of this lesson is well supported by data, and which part is not?
?
Well supported: volume and volatility cluster around session opens and data releases (you can measure it). Weakly supported: quarterly theory and exact AMD timing on every day.


## 5.7 [[5.7 An ICT Model Setup, Step by Step|An ICT Model Setup, Step by Step]]

Your account is 5,000 USD. Same entry and stop. How many units for 1% risk?
?
Risk = 50 USD. 50 ÷ 3.35 = 14.9 → 14 units (real risk 46.90 USD).

After the half at +2R, price comes back to the entry. Why is this a +0.97R trade and not a loss?
?
Half the position was already closed at +2R, and the stop on the rest was moved to break-even, so the second half closed at zero.

Over 20 trades the model wins 6 at +2.5R and loses 14 at −1R. What is the expectancy?
?
6 × 2.5 = 15R; 14 × 1 = 14R; net +1R ÷ 20 = +0.05R per trade, and probably negative after costs. Not good enough yet.

Name the eight steps of the model in order.
?
HTF bias → draw on liquidity (target) → discount/premium → liquidity taken (sweep) → time (killzone) → displacement + MSS → entry at the PD array (FVG CE / OB) → risk (stop beyond the sweep, size for 1R, R:R ≥ 2).

A setup passes seven steps, but the R:R to the target is 1.4. What do you do?
?
Skip it. A missing step is the filter working, not bad luck.


## 5.8 [[5.8 Checkpoint- Phase 5 Review|Checkpoint: Phase 5 Review]]

A bullish dealing range on EUR/USD runs from 1.0800 to 1.0920. Where is equilibrium, and where is the OTE zone for longs (62–79%)?
?
Range = 0.0120. Equilibrium = **1.0860**. OTE = 1.0920 − 0.62 × 0.0120 = **1.0846** down to 1.0920 − 0.79 × 0.0120 = **1.0825** (see 5.3).

Gold: candle 1 high 2,341.0, candle 2 a big up candle, candle 3 low 2,346.4. What's the FVG and its CE? Entry at the CE, stop 2,336.0 below the sweep low, target the buy-side liquidity at 2,371.0. R:R?
?
FVG **2,341.0–2,346.4**, CE **2,343.7**. Risk 7.7, reward 27.3 → about **3.5R** (see 5.4, 5.7).

It's northern summer. When are the London and New York AM killzones in Bangkok time?
?
London **13:00–16:00**; New York AM **18:00–21:00** (one hour later in northern winter) (see 5.6).

Put the model's steps in order: MSS, liquidity target, sweep, bias, PD array, discount, time, risk.
?
Bias → liquidity target → discount → sweep → time → MSS → PD array → risk (see 5.7).

Price wicks below equal lows and closes back above, but there's no displacement and no MSS. Do you buy?
?
No. A sweep without confirmation is not a trade; it may simply be a run that continues lower (see 5.2).
