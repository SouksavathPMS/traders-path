## 9.1 [[9.1 Statistics for Traders|Statistics for Traders]]

Five trades: +1, +1, +1, +1, −6. What are the mean and the median, and what does the difference tell you?
?
Mean = −2 ÷ 5 = −0.4R; median = +1R. Most trades win small, but one big loss wipes them out: negative skew, typical of strategies without tight stops.

A strategy has mean +0.4R and SD 2R over 25 trades. Is the average clearly above zero?
?
Standard error = 2 ÷ √25 = 0.4R; 95% range ≈ 0.4 ± 0.78 → −0.38R to +1.18R. Not yet clearly above zero.

Over the last year, Bitcoin and a tech stock rose and fell together almost every day. Can you hedge one with the other next year?
?
Not safely. Correlation describes the past and can change, often in a crisis exactly when you need it. It also doesn't mean one causes the other.

Why is the median sometimes more useful than the mean for describing trades?
?
Because a few extreme results pull the mean but not the median; the median shows what a normal trade looks like.

How many trades do you need before the 95% range of a 50% win rate is narrower than ±10 percentage points?
?
About 100 (1.96 × √(0.25 ÷ 100) ≈ 0.098).


## 9.2 [[9.2 Probability & Expected Value|Probability & Expected Value]]

A setup wins 30% at +3R and loses 70% at −1R. What's the EV?
?
0.30 × 3 − 0.70 × 1 = 0.90 − 0.70 = +0.20R per trade (before costs).

You've lost five trades in a row with a 45% win-rate setup. Is the next trade more likely to win?
?
No. If trades are independent, the probability is still about 45%. "A win is due" is the gambler's fallacy.

After 20 trades a trader with a real +0.35R edge is at −0.10R per trade. Is the edge gone?
?
Not necessarily. 20 trades is far too few; running averages swing widely early on and converge only over hundreds of trades.

Write the EV formula and calculate it for: 40% at +2.5R, 15% at +0.8R, 10% at 0R, 35% at −1.05R.
?
EV = Σ p × result = 1.00 + 0.12 + 0 − 0.3675 ≈ +0.75R.

Why does a tighter stop make costs more dangerous?
?
Costs are fixed in price terms (pips, cents), so they are a bigger fraction of a smaller risk: 1.9 pips is 0.19R on a 10-pip stop but under 0.05R on a 40-pip stop.


## 9.3 [[9.3 Backtesting Without Fooling Yourself|Backtesting Without Fooling Yourself]]

A test buys at today's open when today's close is above the 20-day average. What's wrong?
?
Look-ahead bias: at the open you can't know today's close. Signal on today's close, trade at tomorrow's open.

You test a strategy on the 30 stocks in today's index over 20 years. Which bias, and why does it flatter the result?
?
Survivorship bias: companies that failed or were removed from the index are missing, so the test only includes the winners.

The best in-sample pair was SMA 45/240, but its neighbours (40/240, 45/220, 50/260) all lost money. Trust it?
?
No. A lone sharp peak surrounded by losers is the signature of overfitting. Prefer parameter regions where many neighbours work (a plateau).

You run the out-of-sample test, don't like the result, change one parameter and run it again. Is it still out-of-sample?
?
No. Once you adjust after seeing it, that data has been used to choose the rules; it's now in-sample.

Name the five biases from the figure and one fix for each.
?
Look-ahead: signal on bar t, trade on t+1. Survivorship: include delisted symbols. Overfitting: few parameters, plateaus, out-of-sample. Data snooping: count ideas tried, demand stronger evidence. Ignoring costs: charge realistic costs and slippage on every trade.

What is the most honest test of a strategy, and why?
?
A forward test in real time (paper or small size): the data didn't exist when the rules were written, so no hindsight can leak in.


## 9.4 [[9.4 Tick Data & Market Internals ($TICK)|Tick Data & Market Internals ($TICK)]]

The S&P 500 is up 0.6%, but $ADD is −400. What does that suggest?
?
A narrow rally: a few heavyweight stocks are lifting the index while most stocks fall. Less trustworthy, especially for breakouts.

On a strong up day, $TICK hits +1,000 three times and price keeps rising. Should you short each +1,000 reading?
?
No. In a trend, extremes in the trend direction show strength. Only fade an extreme at a level where price stalls and structure shifts.

The index breaks above yesterday's high, $ADD is +1,700 and $VOLD strongly positive. How should that change your view of the breakout?
?
It supports the breakout: broad participation (initiative buying). You can trust it more and use trend tactics.

Define $TICK, $ADD and $TRIN in one line each.
?
$TICK: NYSE stocks on an uptick minus those on a downtick, right now. $ADD: advancing minus declining stocks today. $TRIN: (advancers ÷ decliners) ÷ (up volume ÷ down volume); below 1 bullish.

Where does a −1,000 $TICK reading matter most?
?
At a pre-marked support level where price stops falling: a possible capitulation or absorption, to be confirmed by a price trigger.


## 9.5 [[9.5 A Simple Quant Strategy in Python|A Simple Quant Strategy in Python]]

The script prints "bars: 2016 in-sample: 1411 out-of-sample: 605". What do those three numbers mean?
?
2,016 daily prices in total; the first 1,411 (70%) were used to choose the moving-average settings; the last 605 (30%) were kept back to judge them.

Why does the code use `held = pos[i - 1]` and not `pos[i]`?
?
The signal is computed from day i−1's close, so it can only be traded from day i onward. Using pos[i] would trade on information not yet available (look-ahead bias).

In-sample the strategy beat buy and hold; out-of-sample it didn't. Which result should guide your expectations, and why?
?
Out-of-sample: the settings were chosen to look good in-sample, so that part is flattered. Unseen data is the honest estimate (and even it is based on only 3 trades).

Name the three honesty rules built into the script.
?
No look-ahead (signal on day t traded on day t+1), costs on every position change, and settings chosen on the first 70% only, judged on the last 30%.

What is the single biggest weakness of this test's conclusion?
?
Far too few trades (8 in-sample, 3 out-of-sample) on one synthetic series. Nothing can be concluded about edge from that sample.


## 9.6 [[9.6 Performance Metrics- Sharpe, Sortino, Profit Factor|Performance Metrics: Sharpe, Sortino, Profit Factor]]

Trades: +3, −1, −1, −1, +2. What are the profit factor and the max drawdown in R?
?
PF = 5 ÷ 3 ≈ 1.67. Equity: 3, 2, 1, 0, 2 → max drawdown from 3 to 0 = 3R.

An account doubles in 7 years. Roughly what is the CAGR?
?
2^(1/7) − 1 ≈ 10.4% a year.

A backtest shows Sharpe 3.5 and profit factor 6 over 400 trades. What should you check first?
?
Bugs and biases: look-ahead, missing costs, survivorship. Numbers that good with many trades are usually a mistake, not an edge.

What does Sortino measure that Sharpe doesn't?
?
It counts only downside volatility (losses) as risk, so strategies with big winning swings aren't penalised for them.

Why must you always note the number of trades next to a metric?
?
Metrics from few trades are dominated by luck; the same profit factor or Sharpe means far more over 300 trades than over 10.


## 9.7 [[9.7 Checkpoint- Phase 9 Review|Checkpoint: Phase 9 Review]]

A setup wins 22 of 40 trades (55%). Roughly what range could the true win rate be in (95%)?
?
± 1.96 × √(0.55 × 0.45 ÷ 40) ≈ ±0.15 → about **40% to 70%**. Not yet enough to call it better than 50% (see 9.1).

A trade has a 35% chance of +2.5R, 25% chance of +0.3R and 40% chance of −1R, with costs of 0.05R per trade. EV?
?
0.35 × 2.5 + 0.25 × 0.3 − 0.40 × 1 − 0.05 = 0.875 + 0.075 − 0.40 − 0.05 = **+0.50R** (see 9.2).

80 trades: 30 winners averaging +1.8R, 50 losers at −1R. Profit factor and expectancy?
?
Gross wins 54R, gross losses 50R → profit factor **1.08**; expectancy (54 − 50) ÷ 80 = **+0.05R**: barely positive, and costs could erase it (see 9.6).

Name three ways a backtest can lie, and the fix for each.
?
For example: look-ahead bias (only use data available at the decision time); ignoring costs (include spread, commission, slippage); overfitting (keep an out-of-sample period, use walk-forward) (see 9.3).

Why must every performance metric come with a trade count?
?
Small samples give extreme, unreliable numbers (e.g. profit factor 12 from 3 trades). The trade count tells you how much luck could be in the result.
