## 0.1 [[0.1 Why Exchanges & Brokers Exist|Why Exchanges & Brokers Exist]]

Exchange::an organised, regulated marketplace (NYSE, Nasdaq, CME, SET, LSX) that matches buy and sell orders.
Matching engine::the exchange computer that pairs buyers and sellers by price and time *(see 1.2 order book)*.
Broker::a licensed company that gives you an account and sends or fills your orders.
Clearing house::the body that guarantees both sides of an exchange trade pay and deliver.
Custodian::the institution that holds your assets (shares, cash) in your name.
Counterparty::whoever is on the other side of your trade.
OTC (over the counter)::trading directly with a dealer instead of on an exchange.
CFD (contract for difference)::a contract with your broker that pays the price change of an asset, without you owning the asset *(see 0.2)*.
Liquidity provider (LP)::a bank or large market maker that supplies prices to brokers.
Regulator::the government body that licenses and supervises brokers (e.g. Thai SEC, US SEC, UK FCA).
Segregated account::client money kept separate from the broker's own money.
Spread / commission / swap::the three main trading costs: bid-ask gap, fixed fee per trade, and overnight holding fee.
Slippage::getting filled at a worse price than you clicked.
KYC::"Know Your Customer": the ID checks a real broker must do.

## 0.2 [[0.2 Instruments & Leverage- Spot, CFD, Futures, ETF|Instruments & Leverage: Spot, CFD, Futures, ETF]]

Underlying (asset)::the real thing whose price a product follows: gold, the S&P 500 index, Bitcoin, a currency pair.
Spot::buying the underlying itself for immediate delivery; you own it.
CFD (contract for difference)::a contract with your broker that pays you (or charges you) the change in price; you never own the asset *(see 0.1)*.
Futures contract::an exchange contract to buy or sell a fixed amount of an asset at a set price on a set future date.
Expiry / contract month::the date a futures or option contract ends.
Roll::closing a futures contract that is about to expire and opening the same position in the next month.
ETF (exchange-traded fund)::a fund that holds assets (stocks, gold) and whose shares trade on a stock exchange like a normal share.
Option::the right, but not the obligation, to buy or sell at a fixed price before a date *(see 8.1)*.
Counterparty::whoever is on the other side of your trade.
Position value (notional)::the full market value your trade controls: size × price.
Leverage::controlling a position bigger than your deposit; 1:100 means 1 USD controls 100 USD.
Margin::the part of your money the broker or exchange locks as a deposit for a leveraged position.
Initial margin / maintenance margin::(futures) the deposit to open a contract / the minimum you must keep while it is open.
Equity::your balance plus or minus the profit or loss of open trades, right now.
Margin level::equity ÷ used margin × 100%.
Margin call / stop out::a warning when your margin level falls too low / the broker force-closing your trades.
Negative-balance protection::a rule that your account cannot go below zero; the broker absorbs the extra loss.
Spread / commission / swap::bid-ask gap / fee per trade / overnight financing charge *(see 0.1)*.
Triple swap::the one night a week when three nights of swap are charged at once.
Rollover::the moment the trading day ends for the broker (about 04:00 UTC+7; 05:00 in northern winter) and swap is charged.
Contango / backwardation::futures for later months cost more / less than today's price.
CME (Chicago Mercantile Exchange) / COMEX (Commodity Exchange)::the US exchange group where most index and gold futures trade; COMEX is its metals exchange.
MES / MGC::Micro E-mini S&P 500 futures / Micro Gold futures: small contracts for small accounts.
Expense ratio::the yearly fee an ETF takes, as a % of your holding.
Leveraged / inverse ETF::an ETF that aims for 2× or 3× the daily move / the opposite of the daily move.
SPY, QQQ, GLD::ticker symbols of three large ETFs: S&P 500, Nasdaq-100 (the 100 biggest non-financial Nasdaq companies), and gold.

## 0.3 [[0.3 Forex- How Currencies Trade|Forex: How Currencies Trade]]

Currency pair::two currencies quoted against each other, e.g. EUR/USD.
Base currency::the first one (EUR); the thing you buy or sell.
Quote currency::the second one (USD); what the price is measured in.
Pip::the standard small price step: 0.0001 for most pairs, 0.01 for pairs with JPY.
Pipette::one tenth of a pip (the 5th decimal on most platforms).
Lot::trade size. Standard lot = 100,000 units of the base currency; mini = 10,000; micro = 1,000.
Pip value::how much money one pip is worth for your position size.
Leverage::trading a position bigger than your deposit; 1:100 means 1 USD controls 100 USD.
Margin::the deposit the broker locks to open a leveraged position.
Free margin / margin level::money still available / equity ÷ used margin × 100%.
Margin call / stop out::broker warning when losses eat your margin / broker force-closes your trades.
Swap (rollover)::the overnight interest you pay or earn for holding a position.
Majors / minors / exotics::pairs with USD and big economies / big pairs without USD / pairs with smaller-economy currencies (e.g. USD/THB).
Central bank::the institution that sets a country's interest rate (Fed, ECB, BoJ, Bank of Thailand).
DXY::the US Dollar Index: the dollar measured against six major currencies.

## 0.4 [[0.4 Gold (XAU-USD)|Gold (XAU/USD)]]

XAU/USD::the price of one troy ounce of gold in US dollars. XAU is the international code for gold, like USD for the dollar.
Troy ounce (oz)::the unit for precious metals: **31.1035 grams** (heavier than a kitchen ounce).
Spot gold::the price for (almost) immediate delivery of gold; what XAU/USD CFDs follow.
LBMA (London Bullion Market Association)::the trade body of the London gold market, the world's main wholesale gold market.
LBMA Gold Price::the official benchmark price, set in two auctions each day (AM and PM).
COMEX / GC::the US metals exchange / its gold futures contract (100 oz) *(see 0.2)*.
GLD::the largest gold ETF; each share is backed by gold in a vault *(see 0.2)*.
Purity (fineness)::how much of a bar is pure gold. Thai bars are **96.5%**; international bars 99.5% or more.
Baht-weight (บาท)::the Thai gold unit: **15.244 g** for bars (ornaments use 15.16 g).
GTA (Gold Traders Association of Thailand)::the association that announces the reference Thai gold price several times a day.
Making fee (ค่ากำเหน็จ)::the extra charge for crafting gold jewellery (ornaments).
Nominal yield::the interest rate a government bond pays, before inflation.
Inflation expectations::how much inflation the market expects over the coming years.
Real yield::nominal yield minus expected inflation: what a safe bond really earns.
Basis point (bp)::one hundredth of a percent: 0.01%. A rise from 4.20% to 4.30% is +10 bp.
CPI (Consumer Price Index)::the main monthly measure of inflation *(see 0.8)*.
Fed (Federal Reserve)::the US central bank, which sets US interest rates.
DXY::the US Dollar Index *(see 0.3)*.
Safe haven::an asset people buy when they are scared (gold, US dollar, Japanese yen, Swiss franc).
Reserves::the money and assets a central bank holds (dollars, euros, gold).
Tonne (t)::1,000 kg, about 32,150 troy ounces. Central-bank buying is reported in tonnes.
ATR (average true range)::an indicator showing how far price usually moves in one candle (e.g. ATR(14) = average of the last 14 candles).
Lot (gold CFD)::on most CFD brokers 1 lot = 100 oz, but this differs: check the contract specification.

## 0.5 [[0.5 Stocks & Indices|Stocks & Indices]]

Share (stock)::one small unit of ownership in a company. A **shareholder** owns shares.
Ticker::the short code of a stock or fund: NVDA (Nvidia), AAPL (Apple), PTT (on SET).
IPO (initial public offering)::the first time a company sells shares to the public.
Primary / secondary market::the company selling new shares (IPO) / investors trading existing shares with each other on an exchange (almost all trading).
Dividend::a part of the profit paid to shareholders in cash.
Ex-dividend date::from this day, new buyers no longer get the next dividend; the price usually drops by about the dividend.
Buyback::the company buys its own shares and cancels them, so each remaining share owns a bigger slice.
Stock split::cutting each share into several cheaper ones (e.g. 1 share at 200 → 2 shares at 100); the value you own doesn't change.
Market capitalisation (market cap)::the total value of a company on the stock market: share price × number of shares.
Revenue::all the money a company takes in from sales.
Earnings (profit, net income)::what is left after all costs and taxes.
EPS (earnings per share)::profit ÷ number of shares.
Guidance::the company's own forecast for the next quarter or year.
P/E (price-to-earnings ratio)::share price ÷ EPS: how many years of today's profit you pay for.
Present value / discounting::what a future payment is worth today, after allowing for interest *(see 10.6)*.
Index::one number that tracks a basket of stocks (S&P 500, Nasdaq-100, Dow, SET50).
Market-cap weighted / price weighted::bigger companies count more / higher-priced shares count more.
S&P 500::500 large US companies, market-cap weighted.
Nasdaq-100::the 100 largest non-financial companies on the Nasdaq exchange; heavy in technology.
Dow (Dow Jones Industrial Average)::30 large US companies, price weighted.
SET / SET50::the Stock Exchange of Thailand / an index of 50 large, liquid Thai stocks.
LSX / LSX Composite::the Lao Securities Exchange / its main index.
ETF, futures, CFD::wrappers for trading an index *(see 0.2)*. **ES / MES** = E-mini / Micro E-mini S&P 500 futures.
Pre-market / after-hours::trading before the official open / after the close, with fewer traders and wider spreads.
Gap::when a candle opens far from the previous close, with no trades in between.
Earnings season::the weeks each quarter when most companies report results.
Rebalance::when an index changes its members or weights, and funds that track it must buy and sell.
OPEX (options expiration)::the day options contracts expire, usually the third Friday of the month *(see 8.5)*.
Sector::a group of companies in the same kind of business (technology, banks, energy, healthcare…).
Cyclical / defensive::sectors that boom and bust with the economy / that stay steady because people need them anyway.
Rotation::money moving from one sector to another as the economy changes *(see 10.1)*.

## 0.6 [[0.6 Case Study- Nvidia|Case Study: Nvidia]]

NVDA::Nvidia's ticker on the Nasdaq exchange.
GPU (graphics processing unit)::a chip that does many small calculations at the same time; first for game graphics, now for AI.
AI accelerator::a chip built to speed up AI work (Nvidia's data-centre GPUs).
Data centre::a building full of servers that runs cloud services and AI.
Hyperscaler::one of the giant cloud companies (Amazon, Microsoft, Alphabet/Google, Meta, Oracle) that buy chips in huge quantities.
Capex (capital expenditure)::money a company spends on long-lasting things like buildings, servers and chips.
Fiscal year (FY)::a company's own accounting year. Nvidia's fiscal 2027 runs from about February 2026 to January 2027, so "Q2 FY2027" is the quarter that ended on 26 July 2026.
Revenue / gross margin::total sales / the % of sales left after the direct cost of making the product.
Guidance::the company's forecast for next quarter *(see 0.5)*.
Consensus / beat / miss::the average analyst forecast / results above it / below it.
Export controls::government rules that limit which chips can be sold to which countries.
TSMC (Taiwan Semiconductor Manufacturing Company)::the factory ("foundry") that actually manufactures Nvidia's chips.
Straddle::buying a call and a put at the same strike and expiry; profits from a big move either way *(see 8.1)*.
Implied move::the size of move the options market is pricing for an event, roughly the straddle price ÷ stock price.
IV (implied volatility)::how much movement option prices assume *(see 8.2)*.
IV crush::the sharp fall in option prices right after an event, once the uncertainty is gone.
Beta::how much a stock tends to move compared with the whole market; beta 2 = about twice as much.
Trailing / forward P/E::price ÷ profit of the last 12 months / price ÷ profit analysts expect over the next 12 months *(see 0.5)*.
ATR (average true range)::how far price usually moves in one candle *(see 0.4)*.
Concentration risk::having too much of your money depend on one company or one theme.
GEX (gamma exposure) / call wall / put wall::how options dealers' hedging can push or hold price at certain strikes *(see 8.4, 8.5)*.

## 0.7 [[0.7 Crypto- Bitcoin, Ether & Stablecoins|Crypto: Bitcoin, Ether & Stablecoins]]

Cryptocurrency (crypto)::a digital asset recorded on a blockchain, not issued by a government.
Blockchain::a shared record (ledger) of transactions, copied and checked by many computers.
Bitcoin (BTC)::the first and largest cryptocurrency; maximum supply 21 million coins.
Mining / block reward::computers compete to add the next page (block) to the Bitcoin ledger and are paid newly created bitcoins.
Halving::every ~4 years (210,000 blocks) the Bitcoin block reward is cut in half.
Ether (ETH) / Ethereum::the coin / the blockchain that can run programs.
Smart contract::a program on a blockchain that runs automatically when its conditions are met.
Stablecoin::a token designed to stay at 1 USD (e.g. **USDT** from Tether, **USDC** from Circle), backed by reserves.
Reserves::the cash and short-term government bonds a stablecoin company holds to pay back holders.
Altcoin::any cryptocurrency other than Bitcoin.
CEX (centralised exchange)::a company that runs an order book and holds customers' coins (e.g. a licensed Thai exchange).
DEX (decentralised exchange)::trading through smart contracts directly from your own wallet, with no company in the middle.
Wallet::software or a device that holds your keys.
Private key / seed phrase::the secret that controls your coins / the 12–24 words that can rebuild it. Whoever has it owns the coins.
Custody::who holds the keys: you (self-custody) or a company (custodial).
Hardware wallet::a small device that keeps your private key offline.
2FA (two-factor authentication)::a second login check, e.g. a code from an authenticator app.
Spot Bitcoin / Ether ETF::an exchange-traded fund that holds real bitcoin or ether *(see 0.2)*.
Perpetual futures (perps)::crypto futures with **no expiry**, kept close to the spot price by a **funding rate**.
Funding rate::a regular payment between longs and shorts on a perp (often every 8 hours).
Liquidation::the exchange force-closing a leveraged position because the margin is used up *(like a stop out, see 0.2)*.
Liquidation cascade / long squeeze::forced selling that pushes price into more liquidations, again and again.
On-chain data::information read directly from the blockchain: transfers, exchange balances, active addresses.
UTC::Coordinated Universal Time; UTC+7 is Thailand/Laos time.

## 0.8 [[0.8 What Moves Markets- News & the Economic Calendar|What Moves Markets: News & the Economic Calendar]]

Economic calendar::a schedule of upcoming data releases and central-bank events (free on sites such as Forex Factory or Investing.com).
Actual / Forecast (consensus) / Previous::the released number / the average expert guess / last period's number.
Revision::a change to the *previous* number; sometimes as important as the new one.
Surprise::actual − forecast. The size and direction of the surprise drives the reaction.
Priced in::already reflected in the price because everyone expected it.
CPI::Consumer Price Index: inflation. **Core CPI** excludes food and energy.
PCE::Personal Consumption Expenditures price index: the inflation measure the Fed prefers.
NFP::Non-Farm Payrolls: new US jobs last month (released with the unemployment rate and wages).
GDP::Gross Domestic Product: total economic output; growth.
PMI / ISM::surveys of business managers; above 50 = expanding, below 50 = shrinking.
FOMC::the Fed committee that sets US interest rates (8 scheduled meetings a year).
Fed, ECB, BoE, BoJ, BoT::central banks of the US, euro area, UK, Japan, Thailand.
Earnings season::the weeks when companies report quarterly results. **EPS** = earnings per share. **Guidance** = the company's forecast for the next quarter.
Risk-on / risk-off::markets in a buying-risk mood (stocks, crypto, AUD up) vs a fearful mood (USD, JPY, gold, bonds up).
Gap::a jump between one candle's close and the next candle's open, often over a weekend.

## 0.9 [[0.9 Staying Safe- Scams, Fake Brokers & Signal Groups|Staying Safe: Scams, Fake Brokers & Signal Groups]]

Scam / fraud::deceiving someone to take their money.
Clone broker::a fake company copying the name, logo and licence number of a real, licensed firm.
Account manager::a "personal adviser" who calls you; at scam brokers, a salesperson paid to make you deposit more.
Deposit bonus::extra "free" money added to your account, usually with conditions that block withdrawals.
Signal group::a chat group (LINE, Telegram) selling trade ideas ("buy gold now").
Copy trading::automatically copying another trader's trades.
Ponzi scheme::paying "returns" to old members with new members' money; it collapses when new money stops.
Pyramid scheme::earning mainly by recruiting others.
Pig-butchering::a long scam where the criminal builds a friendship or romance for weeks ("fattening the pig") before leading the victim to a fake investment app.
Recovery scam::someone who promises to get your lost money back for a fee, often the same criminals.
Phishing::fake messages or websites that steal passwords or codes.
Regulator / register::the official supervisor of financial firms / its public list of licensed firms *(see 0.1)*.
SEC Thailand (ก.ล.ต.)::Thailand's Securities and Exchange Commission.
LSCO::Lao Securities Commission Office.
FCA / ASIC / MAS::regulators of the UK / Australia / Singapore.
Track record::a history of past trades or returns.
Survivorship bias::only seeing the winners because the losers stopped posting *(see 9.3)*.
AOC 1441::Thailand's Anti-Online Scam Operation Center hotline.

## 0.11 [[0.11 Personal Finance First- Emergency Fund, Debt & Money You Can Afford to Risk|Personal Finance First: Emergency Fund, Debt & Money You Can Afford to Risk]]

Budget::a list of your monthly income and spending, so you know where the money goes.
Essential expenses::costs you must pay every month: rent, food, transport, bills, family support, insurance.
Emergency fund::3–6 months of essential expenses kept in cash, never invested *(see 10.7, 11.2)*.
High-interest debt::credit cards, personal loans, buy-now-pay-later and informal loans that charge well above what investments usually earn.
Interest rate (per year)::the cost of borrowing, as a % of the balance per year.
Minimum payment::the smallest amount a lender lets you pay each month; paying only this keeps you in debt for years.
Compounding::interest charged on earlier interest, which makes debt grow faster *(see 3.1)*.
Investable money::savings left after the emergency fund and money needed within a few years.
Risk capital::the part of your money you could lose completely without changing your life; the only money for trading.
1R::the money you lose if one trade hits its stop *(see 3.3)*.

## 0.12 [[0.12 Short Selling- How It Works, Borrow Fees & Short Squeezes|Short Selling: How It Works, Borrow Fees & Short Squeezes]]

Short selling (going short)::selling borrowed shares (or a contract) to profit if the price falls; you buy back later to close.
Cover / buy to close::buying back to close a short position.
Borrow (stock loan)::your broker borrows the shares from another investor's account.
Borrow fee::the yearly rate you pay for the loan, charged daily.
Easy-to-borrow / hard-to-borrow (HTB)::shares that are cheap and plentiful to borrow / scarce and expensive.
General collateral (GC) rate::the standard low fee for easy-to-borrow shares.
Recall::the lender asking for the shares back, which can force you to close.
Short interest::the number of shares currently sold short, often shown as a % of the free float.
Free float::shares available to trade (not locked up by insiders or governments).
Days to cover::short interest ÷ average daily volume: how many days of normal trading it would take for shorts to buy back.
Short squeeze::a fast rise that forces short sellers to buy back, pushing the price higher.
Uptick rule::a rule allowing short sales only at a price above the last trade.
Naked short selling::selling shares without borrowing them first; illegal in most markets.

## 0.13 [[0.13 TradingView Basics- Charts, Drawing Tools, Indicators & Alerts|TradingView Basics: Charts, Drawing Tools, Indicators & Alerts]]

Symbol::the code for a market, e.g. `EURUSD`, `XAUUSD`, `NVDA`, `PTT`. Often written `EXCHANGE:SYMBOL`, e.g. `SET:PTT`.
Timeframe::how much time one candle covers: 5 minutes, 1 hour, 1 day, 1 week *(see 1.5)*.
Drawing tools::lines, rectangles, Fibonacci and measuring tools you place on the chart.
Long / short position tool::a drawing that shows entry, stop and target, and calculates risk:reward and size.
Indicator::a calculation drawn on the chart, such as a moving average or RSI *(see 2.8)*.
Alert::a message (pop-up, app, email) sent when price or an indicator meets a condition you set.
Watchlist::your list of symbols to follow, with live price changes.
Linear / log scale::a price axis where equal distances mean equal points (linear) or equal percentages (log).
Delayed data::prices shown some minutes late because the exchange charges for real-time data; often marked **D**.
Bars of history::how many candles back the platform will load on one chart.

## 0.14 [[0.14 Economic Calendars & Stock Screeners in Practice|Economic Calendars & Stock Screeners in Practice]]

Economic calendar::a list of upcoming data releases and central-bank events with times, forecasts and results *(see 0.8)*.
Importance / impact::the calendar's rating (often 1–3 bulls, stars or colours) of how much an event usually moves markets.
Actual / Forecast / Previous::the released number, the economists' average expectation (consensus), and last period's number.
Revised::last period's number after it was corrected; it can matter as much as the new number.
Time-zone setting::the calendar option that shows every time in your own zone (choose UTC+7).
Daylight saving time (DST)::clocks moving one hour forward in summer in the US and Europe; Thailand and Laos never change.
Screener::a tool that filters a whole market by rules you choose (size, valuation, trend, events).
Market capitalisation::share price × number of shares: the size of the company on the stock market.
Liquidity filter::a minimum volume or traded value, so you can enter and exit without moving the price.
Watchlist::the short list the screener leaves you with, to study by hand.

## 0.15 [[0.15 Prop Firms & Funded Challenges- How They Work and the Real Odds|Prop Firms & Funded Challenges: How They Work and the Real Odds]]

Prop firm (proprietary trading firm)::traditionally a company that trades its own money; today the term mostly means firms that **sell evaluation challenges** to retail traders.
Challenge / evaluation / combine::the paid test you must pass before getting a "funded" account.
Profit target::the gain you must reach to pass a phase (e.g. +10%).
Daily loss limit::the most you may lose in one day, measured from a fixed point (e.g. the day's opening balance).
Maximum loss / maximum drawdown::the lowest your account may ever go, either **static** (fixed) or **trailing** (moves up with profits).
Trailing drawdown::a loss floor that rises as your balance rises, but never falls.
Funded account::the account you trade after passing; at most retail prop firms it is still **simulated**.
Profit split::the share of (simulated) profits the firm pays you, e.g. 80%.
Payout::real money the firm sends you from your profit share.
Consistency rule::a limit on how much of your profit may come from one day.
