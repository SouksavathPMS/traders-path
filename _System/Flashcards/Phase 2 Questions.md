## 2.1 [[2.1 Swings- HH, HL, LH, LL|Swings: HH, HL, LH, LL]]

In an uptrend, which legs are the impulses, and which are the pullbacks? How do they usually differ?
?
Up legs are impulses, down legs are pullbacks. Impulses are usually longer and faster; pullbacks shorter and slower.

Highs: 80 → 83 → 81. Lows: 75 → 78 → 79. Label the last high and the last low, then name the state.
?
81 is an **LH** (below 83); 79 is an **HL** (above 78). LH + HL = **contraction**: a squeeze or a range is forming.

A candle makes a new high, and the next candle has a lower high. Can you mark a confirmed swing high yet?
?
No. One candle on the right isn't enough; you need **two** closed candles with lower highs.

What does a higher low tell you about buyers' behaviour?
?
Buyers stepped in earlier, at a higher price than last time: demand is getting more eager.

Highs: 120 → 124 → 127. Lows: 115 → 113 → 110. What's the state, and what should you do?
?
HH + LL = **expansion**: volatile, no side in control. Trade smaller or stand aside.


## 2.2 [[2.2 Trends, Ranges & Market Cycles|Trends, Ranges & Market Cycles]]

Highs: 75.2, 75.0, 75.3. Lows: 71.1, 70.9, 71.0. What's the state, and what is allowed?
?
Highs and lows roughly equal → **range** with a ceiling near 75 and a floor near 71. Buy near the floor, sell near the ceiling, or wait. Never the middle.

Impulse +6, pullback −5. Healthy or tired, and what do you do?
?
Ratio 5 ÷ 6 ≈ 0.83: **tired**. Stop adding, manage open trades tightly, and wait for a break of structure before any reversal idea.

After a long uptrend, price goes sideways for weeks. What are the two possible outcomes, and how will you know which one?
?
Continuation (re-accumulation) or reversal (distribution). The breakout tells you: a close above the range = continuation; a close below = likely markdown.

Name the three states and the main rule for each.
?
Uptrend: buy pullbacks, no shorts. Downtrend: sell rallies, no longs. Range: trade the edges or wait, never the middle.

Range 50–56. You buy at 50.4 with a stop at 49.4. Target 55.4. What's the R:R?
?
Risk 1.0, reward 5.0 → **5R**.


## 2.3 [[2.3 Break of Structure & Change of Character|Break of Structure & Change of Character]]

In a downtrend, price closes above the last lower high that created the latest lower low. BOS or CHoCH?
?
CHoCH: a close beyond the protected swing **against** the downtrend.

After BOS 120, price dips to 111.5 and a candle closes at 111.0, below a minor low at 111.3 inside the pullback. CHoCH?
?
No. Only a close below the protected low (109) counts. Minor lows inside a pullback don't change the trend.

On the 1-hour chart a candle wicks 5 points below the protected low and closes 2 points above it. What do you record?
?
No break: a rejection (possible sweep). The trend's structure is intact on the 1-hour chart.

After a CHoCH down, price rallies and makes a **new high** above the old one. What happened?
?
A false alarm: the uptrend resumed. A new downtrend needs a lower high followed by a BOS down.

Define BOS and CHoCH in one line each, for an uptrend.
?
BOS: a candle close above the last swing high (trend continues). CHoCH: the first candle close below the protected HL (the HL that made the latest HH).

Why is the CHoCH candle usually a poor place to sell?
?
It often closes right into support and is followed by a retest. A lower high and a BOS down give a better-defined entry and stop.


## 2.4 [[2.4 Support, Resistance & Role Reversal|Support, Resistance & Role Reversal]]

Name two groups of traders who add buy orders at a support level, and why.
?
For example: buyers who missed the last bounce (want a second chance), and short sellers whose stops or profit-taking orders sit there (they must buy to close).

A 15m support from 2 years ago, tested 4 times last week, with no confluence. Strong or weak? Why?
?
Weak on almost every factor: small timeframe, old, heavily tested (orders used up), no confluence. Delete it or treat it as minor.

Support at 50 breaks with a daily close at 48. Price rallies back to 49.8. What do you expect, and why?
?
Old support may act as **resistance**: buyers who bought at 50 are now in loss and want to sell at break-even, and missed sellers want a second chance. Watch for a rejection to sell.

Price wicks 0.5 above resistance and closes back below it. Which play is this, and where does the stop go?
?
A **failed break**: sell after the close back inside, stop beyond the wick extreme.

Why can "more touches = stronger" be misleading?
?
Many touches prove the level matters, but each touch fills orders waiting there. A level hit many times in a short period is more likely to break next time.

Resistance zone 74.0–75.0 breaks with a close at 76.5. You buy the retest at 75.0, stop 73.8, target 79.2. What's the R:R?
?
Risk 1.2, reward 4.2 → **3.5R**.


## 2.5 [[2.5 Supply & Demand Zones|Supply & Demand Zones]]

Why is the first return to a zone usually stronger than the third?
?
The first return fills the leftover orders. After that, fewer unfilled orders remain at that price.

A demand zone's base spans bodies up to 50.4 and wicks down to 49.6. Where are the proximal and distal lines?
?
Proximal 50.4 (top of the base bodies); distal 49.6 (lowest wick). The stop goes a little below 49.6.

You're a beginner and the zone is against the HTF trend. Limit order or confirmation entry?
?
Confirmation: wait for an LTF CHoCH inside the zone. You'll miss some trades but avoid many zones that fail.

What three parts does every zone have, and which part do you draw?
?
Leg-in, base, leg-out. You draw the base: proximal at the body edge nearest price, distal at the far wick.

Which three grading factors make you skip a zone if they fail?
?
Departure (no explosive move away), freshness (3rd+ return), and trend (against the HTF without strong compensation).


## 2.6 [[2.6 Multi-Timeframe Structure|Multi-Timeframe Structure]]

The daily chart is pulling back in an uptrend. What does the 1H chart usually show at that time?
?
A small downtrend: LH + LL. The 1H downtrend **is** the daily pullback.

Match each job to a timeframe: trigger, direction, location.
?
Direction → HTF (e.g. daily). Location → MTF (e.g. 4H zone). Trigger → LTF (e.g. 1H CHoCH in the HTF direction).

Daily uptrend. On 1H, price breaks below the last HL (a 1H CHoCH **down**) while still far above any daily zone. Short it?
?
No. That's the start of the daily pullback, against the HTF direction. Wait for price to reach a daily zone and look for a 1H CHoCH **up**.

List the 7 steps of the MTF entry model in order (short names are fine).
?
HTF bias → HTF location → LTF pullback structure → LTF CHoCH → entry (new HL or retest) → stop below the CHoCH low → target the HTF swing high, then trail.

Daily demand 88–90, target 98. A 1H CHoCH gives an entry at 90.2 with a stop at 87.6. What's the R:R?
?
Risk 2.6, reward 7.8 → **3R**.


## 2.8 [[2.8 Indicators- Moving Averages, RSI, MACD, ATR, Volume|Indicators: Moving Averages, RSI, MACD, ATR, Volume]]

Can an indicator know something the candles don't show? Why or why not?
?
No. It's calculated only from past prices and volume, so it can only summarise them differently, with some delay.

Which question does each family answer: trend, momentum, volatility, participation?
?
Trend: which way is the average moving? Momentum: how strong are recent moves? Volatility: how big is a normal move? Participation: how much is trading?

Closes 20, 22, 21, 23, 24. What's the SMA 5?
?
110 ÷ 5 = **22**.

Why does an EMA react faster than an SMA of the same length?
?
It gives more weight to the newest closes, so a new price moves it more.

Average gain 0.8, average loss 0.8. What's RSI?
?
RS = 1 → RSI = 100 − 100 ÷ 2 = **50**.

In a daily up-trend, RSI reaches 75. What does that tell you, and what doesn't it tell you?
?
Buying has been strong recently. It doesn't tell you the trend is ending; structure (a CHoCH) would *(see 2.3)*.

EMA 12 = 50.4, EMA 26 = 51.0, signal = −0.4. MACD and histogram?
?
MACD = **−0.6**; histogram = −0.6 − (−0.4) = **−0.2**.

Previous close 50, high 51, low 47. True range?
?
max(4, 1, 3) = **4**.

A breakout candle trades 0.6× normal volume. Is that strong evidence?
?
No. Low participation; the breakout is more likely to fail. Wait for a retest *(see 2.4)*.

The daily chart is in a clean up-trend and RSI shows a bearish divergence. Do you short?
?
No. Divergence is a warning to stop adding and tighten management; a short needs a structure break (CHoCH) first.

Name the four indicator families and one indicator for each.
?
Trend: SMA/EMA or MACD. Momentum: RSI. Volatility: ATR. Participation: volume.

ATR(14) is 2.4. You use a 1.5 × ATR buffer and risk 120 USD. If the stop distance is just the buffer, what size?
?
Stop = 1.5 × 2.4 = **3.6** → size = 120 ÷ 3.6 ≈ **33 units** *(see 3.2)*.


## 2.9 [[2.9 Classic Chart Patterns- Double Tops, Head & Shoulders, Triangles, Flags|Classic Chart Patterns: Double Tops, Head & Shoulders, Triangles, Flags]]

In structure terms, what is a head-and-shoulders top?
?
A higher high (the head), then a lower high (the right shoulder), then a close below the neckline: a change of character *(see 2.3)*.

A double top forms but price never closes below the neckline. Is it a valid pattern?
?
No. Without confirmation it's just two highs; Bulkowski reports that twin peaks without neckline confirmation fail about 65% of the time.

Inverse H&S: head 80, neckline 92. Height and target?
?
Height 12 → target 92 + 12 = **104**, after a close above 92.

Descending triangle: flat bottom 30, first high 34. Target after a close below 30?
?
Height 4 → target **26**.

Why do flags work best after a long, steep pole?
?
The pole shows strong urgency; a short, tight pause means few traders are taking profit, so the trend is more likely to resume.

Which has the higher failure rate in Bulkowski's data: an ascending triangle breaking up or breaking down?
?
Breaking **down**: 38% vs 17%. Breaks in the direction the pattern "leans" do better.

Why isn't "64% reach the target" the same as a 64% win rate for you?
?
It's measured without stops or time limits on US stocks; with a stop, costs and a different market, your win rate and R will differ.

H&S neckline 110, target 100. Entry on a pullback at 110.0, stop 112.0. R:R?
?
Risk 2.0, reward 10.0 → **5R**.

What confirms a pattern, and why is it essential?
?
A candle close beyond the neckline or trend line. Without it, the shape is just swings, and unconfirmed patterns fail far more often.

A bull flag: pole 40 → 52, breakout at 51, stop at 49.5. Measured target and planned R:R?
?
Pole 12 → target **63**; risk 1.5, reward 12 → **8R** planned. Remember flags fail often, so the real expectancy is far lower.


## 2.7 [[2.7 Checkpoint- Phase 2 Review|Checkpoint: Phase 2 Review]]

Swings in order: H 40, L 37, H 44, L 41, H 47, L 43. What's the state, which low is protected, and what does a close at 42.5 mean?
?
Uptrend (HH + HL). The protected low is **41**, the low the latest HH (47) was built from. A close at 42.5 breaks the newest low (43) but not 41: a deeper pullback, **not** a CHoCH (see 2.3).

Gold support 1,950–1,960 breaks with a daily close at 1,938. Price rallies to 1,952. You sell at 1,950, stop 1,962, target 1,914. Account 3,000 USD, risk 1%. R:R and size? (1 lot = 100 oz)
?
Risk 12, reward 36 → **3R**. Risk budget 30 USD ÷ 12 USD per oz = 2.5 oz → round down to **0.02 lot (2 oz)** → actual risk **24 USD** (see 2.4, 0.4).

Daily uptrend, daily demand 45–47, target 55. A 1H CHoCH up gives an entry at 47.0 with a stop at 44.5. R:R, and size for a 100 USD risk?
?
Risk 2.5, reward 8.0 → **3.2R**; size 100 ÷ 2.5 = **40 units** (see 2.6).

What's the difference between a BOS and a CHoCH?
?
A BOS is a close beyond a swing **in** the trend direction (continuation). A CHoCH is the first close beyond the **protected** swing against the trend (control may be changing).

Why buy the retest of a broken level instead of chasing the breakout?
?
Same idea and stop, but a much smaller risk and a much better R:R (about 6× in lesson 2.4's example). Missing a trade costs less than chasing it.
