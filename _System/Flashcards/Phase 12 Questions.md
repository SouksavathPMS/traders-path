## 12.1 [[12.1 Black Monday 1987|Black Monday 1987]]

How far had the S&P 500 already fallen from its August peak by Friday 16 October?
?
From 336.77 to 282.70: 282.70 ÷ 336.77 − 1 ≈ **−16.1%**.

How long did it take the S&P 500 to regain its pre-crash high?
?
Until **26 July 1989**, about 21 months.

How can an automatic "sell when prices fall" rule make a fall bigger?
?
Its selling pushes prices lower, which triggers more of the same selling: a feedback loop, especially when few buyers are left.

Which of the four traders broke which rules from Phases 0–3?
?
The no-stop trader broke "a stop in the market on every trade" *(3.5)*; the leveraged trader broke the 1% risk rule and the no-borrowing rule *(0.2, 3.1)*. The stopped-out trader followed the rules and lost about 3R because of the fast market.

Your stop is 2% below entry and you risk 1%. In a crash it fills 6% below entry. What do you lose?
?
Position = 1% ÷ 2% = 50% of the account; loss = 50% × 6% = **3% of the account** (3R).

Name two rules that would have protected a trader on Black Monday.
?
Any two of: trade only with the trend (flat by September), small risk per trade, a stop in the market, no leverage, a circuit breaker after losses.


## 12.2 [[12.2 The 2008 Financial Crisis|The 2008 Financial Crisis]]

How long did the S&P 500 take from its 2007 peak to its 2009 low, and from the low back to the old high?
?
Peak October 2007 → low March 2009 (about 17 months); back above the peak in March 2013 (about four more years).

Why does high leverage make a firm fragile?
?
With little own capital, a small fall in asset values wipes it out; lenders then pull back and the firm can't refinance.

An investor had a 60/40 IPS with an emergency fund and no debt. What should they have done in late 2008?
?
Follow the crisis plan: keep contributing, rebalance back to 60/40 (buying stocks as they fell), and never sell the core in panic *(see 11.2)*.

What gain is needed to recover from −56.8%?
?
1 ÷ (1 − 0.568) − 1 ≈ **+131%**.

Give one benefit and one cost of a 40-week trend filter.
?
Benefit: it cut the 2008–09 drawdown from 56% to 20% in this test. Cost: whipsaws, losing on false signals in sideways markets.


## 12.3 [[12.3 The Swiss Franc Shock (2015)|The Swiss Franc Shock (2015)]]

Why did EUR/CHF hardly move between 2011 and 2015?
?
The SNB promised to stop it falling below 1.20 and bought euros to defend the level.

Why did a very quiet market make the shock more dangerous for traders?
?
Small daily ranges encouraged tight stops and large positions, and everyone's stops sat in the same place, below 1.20.

Which broker features from Phase 0 mattered most on this day?
?
Regulation and segregated client money (so the broker's failure doesn't take your deposit), and negative-balance protection (so a gap can't put you in debt) *(see 0.1, 0.2)*.

Long 0.2 lots EUR/CHF at 1.2000, stop 1.1970. The fill comes at 1.0500. Loss in CHF?
?
1,500 pips × 0.2 × 10 = **3,000 CHF**, against a planned 60 CHF.

Give two lessons from the franc shock.
?
Low volatility isn't low risk; size for the gap, not the stop; use brokers with negative-balance protection and segregated funds.


## 12.4 [[12.4 The COVID Crash (2020)|The COVID Crash (2020)]]

How many trading days did the fall from record to low take, and how long until the next record?
?
**23 trading days** (19 Feb → 23 Mar); a new record on **18 Aug 2020**, about five months after the low.

Why can prices recover before the economy does?
?
Markets price expected future cash flows and interest rates; once support arrived and the worst case looked less likely, prices rose ahead of the data *(see 10.1)*.

Why does a circuit breaker matter for your stop?
?
During a halt nothing trades; when trading resumes, the price can be far below your stop, so it fills at the next price available.

S&P 500 from 3,386.15 to 2,237.40: what fall, and what gain was needed to recover?
?
−33.9%; recovery needed 3,386.15 ÷ 2,237.40 − 1 ≈ **+51.3%**.

Name two rules from this course that helped in March 2020.
?
Any two of: drawdown rules (halve at −5%, stop at −10%), circuit breakers after losses, IPS rebalancing, emergency fund, no leverage.


## 12.5 [[12.5 The 2022 Rate Shock|The 2022 Rate Shock]]

Add up the 2022 hikes. Where did the policy rate end the year?
?
0.25 + 0.50 + 0.75 × 4 + 0.50 = **4.25** pp → from 0–0.25% to **4.25–4.50%**.

Why did long-term bonds fall more than short-term bonds in 2022?
?
Their price depends on many years of fixed payments, so a rise in yields reduces their value much more (higher duration).

A 60/40 portfolio returned roughly 0.6 × (−19.4%) + 0.4 × (−13%) in 2022. What's that, and what does it say about diversification?
?
About **−16.8%**. Diversification reduces risk but doesn't remove it; correlations can rise exactly when you need protection.

A bond fund has a duration of 6. Yields rise 1.5 percentage points. Approximate price change?
?
−6 × 1.5 ≈ **−9%**.

Why did stocks and bonds fall together in 2022?
?
Both were hit by the same cause: rising rates to fight inflation, which lowers bond prices and the present value of company profits.


## 12.6 [[12.6 The FTX Collapse (2022)|The FTX Collapse (2022)]]

How many days passed between the CoinDesk article and the bankruptcy filing?
?
2 November → 11 November: **9 days**.

Why was FTT a dangerous form of collateral?
?
Its value depended on confidence in FTX itself, so it collapsed at exactly the moment it was needed.

Which red flags from lesson 0.9 applied to FTX?
?
For example: an unregulated or lightly regulated venue holding customer money, returns and growth that seemed too good, a token issued by the platform itself, and celebrity marketing in place of verifiable safeguards.

What did FTX customers' claims get valued in, and at what date?
?
**US dollars**, at the **petition date** (11 Nov 2022), not in the coins they deposited.

Give two rules that protect you from an exchange failure.
?
Keep long-term holdings in self-custody; keep only trading money on regulated exchanges; avoid platform tokens; test withdrawals.


## 12.7 [[12.7 Silicon Valley Bank (2023)|Silicon Valley Bank (2023)]]

What share of SVB's deposits was above the FDIC insurance limit?
?
About **89%** (151.6 billion of 175.5 billion USD).

If SVB's bonds would eventually repay in full, why were the losses real?
?
Because the bank had to sell them **before** maturity to pay depositors, at prices below what it had paid.

An investor held SVB shares as 20% of their portfolio because "banks are safe". Which course rules did that break?
?
Concentration (no single stock should be that large, see 10.7, 11.2), and treating a leveraged business as "safe" without checking its risks (see 10.6).

You hold 1,800,000 THB in one Thai bank. How much is above the DPA limit?
?
1,800,000 − 1,000,000 = **800,000 THB** uninsured.

Name the two balance-sheet weaknesses that made SVB vulnerable.
?
Long-duration bonds bought at low yields (losses when rates rose) and concentrated, mostly uninsured deposits that could leave instantly.


## 12.8 [[12.8 Checkpoint- Phase 12 Review|Checkpoint: Phase 12 Review]]

Long 0.5 lots of a currency pair, stop 25 pips (10 USD per pip per lot). A policy shock fills the stop 600 pips away. Planned and actual loss?
?
Planned: 0.5 × 10 × 25 = **125 USD**. Actual: 0.5 × 10 × 600 = **3,000 USD**, 24 times the plan (see 12.3).

A portfolio falls 34% (as in March 2020). What gain is needed to recover?
?
0.34 ÷ 0.66 ≈ **+51.5%** (see 12.4).

A 10-year bond paying 2% is bought at 100 when yields are 2%. Yields rise to 4.5%. Using a duration of 9, roughly what's the price change?
?
−9 × 2.5 ≈ **−22.5%** (see 12.5, 12.7).

You keep 2 BTC on an exchange that fails; the claim is valued at 20,000 USD per BTC and you recover 120%. What do you get?
?
2 × 20,000 × 1.20 = **48,000 USD**, in dollars, not bitcoin (see 12.6).

Name three of the five survival rules that appear across these case studies.
?
Any three of: small risk per trade, a real stop in the market, no borrowed money or excess leverage, control of your own assets (custody, insured limits, segregated brokers), a written plan followed under stress.

Which single case study changed your trading plan most, and what did you add?
?
Personal answer. Write the rule you added and the lesson number in your plan's changelog *(see 11.3)*.
