## 7.1 [[7.1 Auction Market Theory- Balance & Imbalance|Auction Market Theory: Balance & Imbalance]]

A pair has traded between 1.0850 and 1.0920 for five days. Balance or imbalance? What's the basic approach?
?
Balance. Fade the edges back toward the middle; don't chase breakouts without acceptance.

Price breaks above a range on low volume and comes back inside within 20 minutes. What is it called, and where is price likely to go?
?
A failed auction (rejection). Price often travels back through the range toward the opposite edge.

Value is 100–104. Price is at 106 and buyers keep lifting offers. Responsive or initiative buying?
?
Initiative buying: they are buying **above** value, betting that value is moving up.

Price drops to 99.5 and buyers step in strongly, pushing it back to 101. Responsive or initiative?
?
Responsive buying: buying **below** value because it's cheap, expecting a return to value.

Name the three AMT variables and what each one does.
?
Price advertises (moves to find the other side), time regulates (how long price stays = how accepted it is), volume validates (high volume = acceptance, low volume = rejection).

In balance, which kind of trader usually wins, and in imbalance?
?
Balance: responsive traders (fading the edges). Imbalance: initiative traders (going with the move).


## 7.2 [[7.2 Volume Profile- POC, VAH, VAL|Volume Profile: POC, VAH, VAL]]

Rows: 50 → 200, 51 → 600, 52 → 900, 53 → 500, 54 → 100 (total 2,300). Where is the POC and what is the value area?
?
POC = 52 (900). 70% of 2,300 = 1,610. Start 900; add 51 (600) → 1,500; add 53 (500) → 2,000 ≥ 1,610. Value area 51–53.

A session rallies hard in the morning, then trades sideways at the top all afternoon. What shape is the profile, and what is a common cause?
?
A P shape: a fast rally, then acceptance at the top. Often short covering.

Yesterday's value area was 102.5–106.2. Today opens at 107.0 and stays above 106.2 for two hours on strong volume. What does that suggest?
?
Initiative buying and value moving higher (imbalance). Yesterday's VAH (106.2) becomes likely support; fading back to the POC is the wrong tool.

Define POC, VAH, VAL, HVN and LVN in one line each.
?
POC: the price with the most volume. VAH/VAL: the top/bottom of the range holding ~70% of volume. HVN: a thick part of the profile where price slows. LVN: a thin part where price moves fast.

Why are LVNs good places for stops **beyond**, but bad places to wait for a reaction?
?
Little trading happened there, so few participants defend those prices; price tends to move through them quickly rather than stop.


## 7.3 [[7.3 Level 2 & The Order Book (DOM)|Level 2 & The Order Book (DOM)]]

Best bid 5,000.25 for 250, best ask 5,000.50 for 300. What is the spread in ticks, and what happens if someone sends a market buy for 400?
?
Spread = 0.25 points = 1 tick. The buy takes all 300 at 5,000.50 and the remaining 100 at the next ask level up, so price ticks higher.

At 4,998.00 the DOM always shows about 100 contracts bid, but 1,600 contracts trade there and price doesn't go lower. What is likely going on?
?
An iceberg: a large hidden buyer showing only ~100 at a time and refilling. Executed volume far larger than the visible size is the giveaway.

Why is spoofing illegal?
?
Because the orders exist only to mislead other traders about supply and demand, without any intention to trade.

What's the difference between what the DOM shows and what actually happened?
?
The DOM shows resting intentions that can be cancelled at any time; executed trades (time and sales, footprint) show what really happened.

You want to read an order book for EUR/USD. Which instrument should you use, and why?
?
Euro FX futures (6E) on CME: they have a central exchange order book. Spot FX has no central book.


## 7.4 [[7.4 Footprint Charts & Delta|Footprint Charts & Delta]]

A row shows **120 × 450**. Which side was more aggressive at that price, and what is the row delta?
?
Buyers: 450 bought at the ask vs 120 sold at the bid. Delta = 450 − 120 = +330.

At 101.00 sells are **600**; at 101.25 buys are **150**. Is there a sell imbalance at 101.00 (3× rule)?
?
Yes: 600 ÷ 150 = 4 ≥ 3.

Price makes a lower low but CVD makes a higher low. What could that mean?
?
A bullish divergence: sellers are less aggressive at the new low, or a passive buyer is absorbing them. Watch for a CHoCH up.

A trader buys 10 with a market order when the bid is 50.00 and the ask is 50.05. Where does it appear on the footprint?
?
In the ask (right) column of the 50.05 row: 0 × 10. Delta +10.

Strong positive delta but price doesn't rise. What might be happening?
?
A large passive seller is absorbing the aggressive buying (absorption, 7.6). Delta shows aggression, not direction.


## 7.5 [[7.5 Liquidity Heatmaps|Liquidity Heatmaps]]

A bright band ends abruptly just before price reaches it, and no large trades print. What happened?
?
The order was pulled (cancelled). There was no real support there.

What do the bubbles show that the bright bands don't?
?
Real executed trades (aggression). Bands only show intentions that can be cancelled.

A band at 62,500 shrinks steadily while big buy bubbles hit it, then price breaks through. Eaten or pulled, and what does it suggest?
?
Eaten: aggressive buyers filled the resting sellers. It suggests strong initiative buying and a likely continuation.

Name the three possible fates of a bright band when price reaches it, and what each means.
?
Holds (trades a lot, stays): real absorbing liquidity. Eaten (shrinks under aggressive trades, then breaks): initiative wins. Pulled (vanishes without trades): fake or nervous liquidity, no support.

Why are large resting bands often useful as **targets** rather than entries?
?
Price tends to be drawn to large liquidity (big participants need it to fill), but you can't know in advance whether the band will hold, so it's safer as a place to take profit than as a place to rely on for support.


## 7.6 [[7.6 Absorption & Exhaustion Setups|Absorption & Exhaustion Setups]]

At a support level, three candles show delta of −1,500, −1,800 and −1,650, but the low stays within 0.05. Effort or result missing?
?
Big effort (heavy selling), no result (price doesn't fall): absorption by a passive buyer.

Price makes three new highs with candle deltas of +900, +500 and +150, then a candle with delta −600. What pattern is this?
?
Exhaustion: shrinking positive delta on new highs, then a delta flip. Look for a sweep and a CHoCH down.

Describe absorption and exhaustion in terms of effort vs result.
?
Absorption: big effort (aggressive volume/delta), little or no result (price doesn't move). Exhaustion: the effort itself fades while price still extends, until it stops.

Why does location matter so much for these signals?
?
Absorption or exhaustion at a level that already matters (HTF zone, VAL/VAH, swept liquidity) shows a real participant defending it. In the middle of nowhere it's usually noise.


## 7.7 [[7.7 Checkpoint- Phase 7 Review|Checkpoint: Phase 7 Review]]

A session's volume by price: 100: 5 · 101: 10 · 102: 20 · 103: 30 · 104: 18 · 105: 12 · 106: 5 (total 100). Find the POC and the 70% value area, adding one row at a time (the bigger neighbour first).
?
POC **103** (30). Add 102 (20) → 50; then 104 (18) → 68; then 105 (12) → 80 ≥ 70. Value area **102–105**: VAL 102, VAH 105 (see 7.2).

At a daily demand zone, three candles show market buys/sells of 900/1,300, 1,100/1,600 and 800/1,500, but the lows hold within two ticks. Deltas, cumulative delta, and the reading?
?
Deltas **−400, −500, −700** → cumulative **−1,600**. Heavy selling with no new lows = **absorption** by a passive buyer. Wait for a price trigger before buying (see 7.4, 7.6).

Delta on a 5-minute candle is +1,200, yet the candle closes with a long upper wick at the session's VAH. What's the likely story?
?
Big effort (aggressive buying), poor result (rejection at value's edge): a passive seller absorbed it. Reversal risk back into value (see 7.1, 7.6).

Explain "effort vs result" in one line.
?
Compare the aggression (volume, delta) with the price move it produced: big effort with little result means someone large is on the other side.

Why do orderflow tools work best on futures and stocks, and poorly on spot forex?
?
Futures and stocks trade on central exchanges with a complete order book and trade data; spot forex has no central exchange, so DOM and footprint data are partial (see 7.3).
