## 10.1 [[10.1 The Business Cycle|The Business Cycle]]

PMI has risen from 46 to 52 over four months, the central bank has stopped cutting, and small caps are outperforming. Which phase?
?
Most likely early cycle (recovery): growth rebounding, policy still supportive, cyclicals and small caps leading.

Is the unemployment rate a leading or lagging indicator, and why does it matter?
?
Lagging: companies lay people off after the slowdown has started. Waiting for unemployment to rise usually means the market has already moved.

Name the four phases and one asset or sector that tends to lead in each.
?
Early: small caps/financials. Mid: technology/broad equities. Late: energy/commodities/staples. Recession: bonds/utilities/healthcare.

A PMI survey has 20% better, 50% same, 30% worse. What's the PMI?
?
20 + ½ × 50 = 45: contraction.


## 10.2 [[10.2 Central Banks, Rates & Liquidity (QE-QT)|Central Banks, Rates & Liquidity (QE/QT)]]

The Fed raises rates by 50 bp. How many percentage points is that?
?
0.50 percentage points (1 bp = 0.01%).

A bond fund has a duration of 6. Yields fall by 0.75 percentage points. Roughly what happens to its price?
?
About +4.5% (−6 × −0.75).

The 2-year yield is 4.8% and the 10-year yield is 4.2%. What shape is the curve and what does it often signal?
?
Inverted (short above long): policy is tight now and markets expect cuts later, often because they expect a slowdown.

What's the difference between QE and QT?
?
QE: the central bank buys bonds with new reserves (adds liquidity, lowers yields). QT: it lets bonds roll off or sells them (removes liquidity, a slow headwind).

Why can a rate cut make stocks fall on the day?
?
If the cut is smaller than expected or comes with signals of fewer future cuts, it's hawkish relative to what was priced in; yields and the dollar can rise.


## 10.3 [[10.3 Economic Data & Event Risk|Economic Data & Event Risk]]

It's US winter. At what time in Bangkok is the US CPI released?
?
08:30 New York + 12 hours = 20:30 UTC+7 (19:30 in US summer).

CPI comes in at 2.8% vs a 2.6% consensus, but last month was 3.2%. Is this a hot or cool surprise?
?
Hot: it's above the consensus (+0.2), even though it's lower than last month. Markets compare with the forecast.

A high-impact release is in 10 minutes and you see a perfect setup. What does the plan say?
?
No new entry until 15–30 minutes after the release. The setup can be re-evaluated once the first spike settles.

What moves price at a data release: the number itself or the surprise? Explain in one line.
?
The surprise: actual minus consensus, because the consensus is already priced in.

Name three high-impact US releases and their Bangkok time in US summer.
?
CPI 19:30, nonfarm payrolls 19:30 (first Friday), FOMC decision 01:00 the next morning (press conference 01:30).


## 10.4 [[10.4 Intermarket Analysis|Intermarket Analysis]]

The dollar index rallies strongly. What tends to happen to gold and to Thai stocks, and why?
?
Both tend to come under pressure: gold is priced in dollars, and a strong dollar tightens funding for emerging markets, so foreign investors often sell. These are tendencies, not laws.

The S&P 500 makes a new high, but high-yield credit spreads are widening. What is this called, and what does it suggest?
?
A divergence: the bond market is getting nervous while stocks are still rising. It doesn't time a top, but it's a reason for caution and smaller size.

Why does the oil price matter so much for Thailand?
?
Thailand is a net energy importer, so higher oil raises inflation, worsens the trade balance and affects energy stocks such as the PTT group.

Name four markets you would put on an intermarket watchlist and what each tells you.
?
For example: US 10-year yield (price of money), DXY (dollar strength), oil (inflation pressure), gold (fear and real yields); add VIX and USD/THB if you invest in Thailand.

A US ETF rose 8% in dollars, while USD/THB went from 36.0 to 34.0. What's your return in baht?
?
1.08 × (34.0 ÷ 36.0) − 1 ≈ **+2.0%**.


## 10.5 [[10.5 How Wall Street Works|How Wall Street Works]]

Your free trading app is paid by a market maker for your orders. What is this called, and what's the conflict?
?
Payment for order flow (PFOF). The broker earns more when you trade more, so the app has a reason to encourage frequent trading, which usually hurts you.

Why are these flows partly predictable?
?
They follow rules and calendars (index methodology, target weights, expiry dates), not opinions, so their timing and direction are known in advance.

An analyst raises a price target right after the stock has risen 20%. What should you conclude?
?
Price targets often follow price. Treat it as information about sentiment, not as an independent forecast; ask who benefits if you act on it.

Name one sell-side and one buy-side player, and how each is paid.
?
Sell side: an investment bank, paid fees for IPOs and M&A (or a broker, paid commissions/spreads). Buy side: a pension fund or ETF, paid management fees and judged on returns against a benchmark.

Give two advantages a small, patient investor has over a large fund.
?
Any two of: no benchmark pressure (can hold cash), small size (no price impact), long horizon, no forced selling without excessive leverage.


## 10.6 [[10.6 Fundamental Analysis & Valuation|Fundamental Analysis & Valuation]]

A company reports rising net income every year, but free cash flow has been negative for five years. What's the concern?
?
Profits aren't turning into cash; it may need heavy investment, or its accounting profits may be of low quality. Owners can only be paid from cash.

Why did raising the discount rate from 8% to 10% cut the DCF value in the table by 26%?
?
Every future cash flow is divided by a bigger number, and most of the value lies far in the future (terminal value), where the effect is largest.

Your value estimate is 41 and the price is 30. What's the margin of safety?
?
(41 − 30) ÷ 41 ≈ **27%**.

Why do higher interest rates lower the value of a business, even if nothing changes in the business?
?
Future cash flows are discounted at a higher rate, so their present value falls.

Company X: market cap 20 bn, debt 5 bn, cash 1 bn, EBITDA 2 bn. What are its EV and EV/EBITDA?
?
EV = 20 + 5 − 1 = **24 bn**; EV/EBITDA = 24 ÷ 2 = **12×**.


## 10.7 [[10.7 Portfolio Construction & Asset Allocation|Portfolio Construction & Asset Allocation]]

Why can a "less optimal" 60/40 portfolio beat 100% stocks in real life?
?
Because an investor who panics and sells in a crash locks in the loss. A mix you can hold through a fall keeps compounding; one you abandon doesn't.

Why should the emergency fund come before the first investment?
?
Without it, an unexpected expense or job loss during a crash forces you to sell investments at the worst time.

In the fee table, how much more does the 0.2% fund leave you after 30 years than the 2.0% fund?
?
About 9.40M − 6.85M ≈ **2.55M THB**, from the same contributions.

Stocks fall 40% and bonds rise 5%. What happens to an 80/20 portfolio?
?
0.8 × (−40%) + 0.2 × 5% = **−31%**.

Give three rules that keep a long-term investor on track.
?
For example: rebalance on a schedule, invest a fixed amount monthly (DCA), keep costs low, keep an emergency fund, and write down what you'll do in a crash.


## 10.9 [[10.9 Index Funds & ETFs- Owning the Whole Market|Index Funds & ETFs: Owning the Whole Market]]

An index fund holds a company that then falls 60%. What does the manager do?
?
Nothing special. The fund keeps holding it at its smaller index weight and sells only if it leaves the index. No one decides to "cut the loser".

You want to invest 2,000 baht every month through your bank app. ETF or index mutual fund?
?
Usually the index mutual fund: small regular amounts, no brokerage account needed. An ETF works too if your broker allows small or fractional orders.

Fund A and fund B track the same index. A charges 0.10% a year, B charges 0.90%. What should you expect over 10 years?
?
A should beat B by roughly 0.8 percentage points a year, about 8% of extra money after 10 years before compounding, because they hold the same shares.

Why does an active fund find it hard to beat its index after fees, even with smart managers?
?
The managers as a group *are* most of the market, so before costs they average the market's return. After fees and trading costs, the average must be below it.

Two S&P 500 funds: one is based in the US, one in Ireland. Why might a Thai investor care?
?
Domicile changes how dividends are taxed on the way to you and whether US estate tax can apply. See 10.12 and 10.14, and check current rules before you buy.

500,000 is in a fund with a 1.2% fee. How much do you pay in fees this year, and how much with a 0.2% fund?
?
500,000 × 0.012 = **6,000**; 500,000 × 0.002 = **1,000**. The 5,000 difference repeats, and compounds, every year.

What does an index fund protect you from, and what doesn't it?
?
It protects you from picking the wrong companies or the wrong manager. It doesn't protect you from the whole market falling.


## 10.10 [[10.10 DCA vs Lump Sum|DCA vs Lump Sum]]

Why is DCA's average cost below the average price?
?
A fixed amount buys more units at low prices and fewer at high prices, so the low prices count more in your average.

Prices rise every month for a year. Which method ends with more money?
?
The lump sum: all the money was invested at the lowest price.

Why does the lump sum win most of the time?
?
Markets have risen in most 12-month periods, so money invested earlier usually had more time to grow.

Our test found 81%, Vanguard about 68%. Name one reason they differ.
?
Vanguard let waiting cash earn interest and used different periods, markets and horizons. Our test paid 0% on cash, which favours the lump sum.

You DCA 5,000 a month at prices 50, 40 and 50. How many units, and what's your average cost?
?
100 + 125 + 100 = **325 units**; 15,000 ÷ 325 ≈ **46.15**, below the average price of 46.67.

What's the strongest reason to choose DCA for a lump sum?
?
Behaviour: if a fall soon after investing would make you sell, spreading the entry helps you stay invested, which matters more than the small average cost.


## 10.11 [[10.11 Bonds- Lending for Interest|Bonds: Lending for Interest]]

A 3-year bond, face 1,000, coupon 5%. List every payment you receive if you hold it to maturity.
?
50 at the end of years 1 and 2, then 50 + 1,000 = 1,050 at the end of year 3. Total **1,150**.

A bond fund has a duration of 6. Yields fall 1 percentage point. Roughly what happens to its price?
?
It rises about **6%**.

You'll need your money in 2 years. Why is a 30-year bond a poor fit?
?
You'd have to sell it in 2 years at whatever price the market gives, and a rise in yields could cost 15% or more. A 2-year bond or deposit pays you back exactly when you need it.

Two 3-year bonds: a government bond yielding 2.3% and an unrated company debenture yielding 7.5%. What is the extra 5.2 points paying you for?
?
The risk that the company can't pay, plus the difficulty of selling an unrated bond early. It isn't free money.

A 5-year bond with a 3% coupon (face 100). Market yields are 5%. Is the price above or below 100, and roughly where?
?
Below 100, because its coupon is less than the market yield: about **91.34**.

Name the two questions to ask before buying any bond.
?
Who am I lending to (can they pay)? For how long (what happens if rates rise before I need the money)?


## 10.12 [[10.12 Dividends & Dividend Investing|Dividends & Dividend Investing]]

A share at 80 pays a dividend of 3.20 a year. What's the yield?
?
3.20 ÷ 80 = **4%**.

EPS is 5 and the dividend is 2. What's the payout ratio, and why does it matter?
?
2 ÷ 5 = **40%**. The company keeps 60% to grow or pay down debt; a payout near or above 100% is hard to sustain.

Fund A's price rose 6% this year and it paid 3% in dividends. Fund B's price rose 8% and it paid nothing. Which had the better total return?
?
A: 6 + 3 = **9%** vs B: **8%**, before tax.

A share's price falls from 60 to 30 and the last yearly dividend was 2.40. What yield does a screener show, and what should you check?
?
2.40 ÷ 30 = **8%**. Check whether earnings and cash flow still cover the dividend; the market may be expecting a cut.

You receive 1,000 USD of US dividends as a Thai resident with a W-8BEN on file. How much arrives?
?
1,000 × (1 − 0.15) = **850 USD**, before any Thai tax.

You hold 2,000 shares at 25 baht; the dividend is 1 baht. Before XD your shares are worth 50,000. Roughly what are your shares and cash worth after XD and payment?
?
Shares ≈ 2,000 × 24 = **48,000**; cash = 2,000 − 10% = **1,800**; total ≈ **49,800**.

Why can a very high dividend yield be a warning?
?
It often appears because the price has fallen on fears that the dividend will be cut or the business is in trouble.


## 10.13 [[10.13 REITs- Owning Property Through the Market|REITs: Owning Property Through the Market]]

Why do REITs need to issue new units or borrow to grow?
?
They must pay out most of their profit, so they keep very little to reinvest.

A Thai REIT owns a mall on 30-year leasehold land with 8 years left. What happens to the asset's value as the years pass?
?
It shrinks towards zero at the end of the lease (unless renewed), so part of each distribution is really your capital coming back.

Why can REITs fall more than the wider stock market in a financial crisis?
?
They use debt, depend on banks to refinance, and their tenants struggle in recessions; the 2008 crisis was centred on property and credit *(see 12.2)*.

A REIT trades at 8 with NAV 10 and pays 0.56 a year. What are the yield and the discount to NAV?
?
Yield = 0.56 ÷ 8 = **7%**; discount = 1 − 8 ÷ 10 = **20%**.

Name two reasons REIT prices tend to fall when interest rates rise.
?
Higher interest costs reduce distributable income, and safer bonds now pay more, so investors demand a higher yield (a lower price) from REITs.


## 10.14 [[10.14 Investing from Thailand & Laos- SET, LSX, US Stocks & Taxes|Investing from Thailand & Laos: SET, LSX, US Stocks & Taxes]]

You want to place an order on the SET during your lunch break at 13:00 Bangkok time. What happens?
?
The market is in its midday break; trading restarts after the afternoon pre-open (around 14:00). Your order may queue for the afternoon open, depending on your broker.

Why might a 5,000,000 kip order on a small LSX stock cost more than the commission suggests?
?
Thin trading means few orders near the current price; buying a lot can push the price up against you (slippage) *(see 0.1)*.

Which route lets you buy a US company in baht through a normal Thai account?
?
A **DR** on the SET (if one exists for that company), or a Thai feeder/index fund.

Why does owning US shares directly matter for estate planning if you're not a US citizen or resident?
?
The IRS treats US shares as US-situs assets. If they exceed **60,000 USD** at death, the estate must file a US estate tax return, and US estate tax can apply (IRS, checked October 2026).

Your net income is 400,000 baht. How much does a 50,000 baht ThaiESG purchase save?
?
Your top rate is 10%, so about **5,000** (tax falls from 17,500 to 12,500).

You sell SET-listed shares at a 100,000 baht profit. What personal income tax do you pay on the gain?
?
None: gains on SET-listed shares are exempt for individuals (as of October 2026).

Net income 1,500,000 baht. What does a 200,000 baht RMF purchase save, assuming your other retirement contributions leave room under the cap?
?
Your top rate is 25%: 200,000 × 0.25 = **50,000** (tax falls from 240,000 to 190,000).

Name three things in this lesson that you should re-check every year.
?
Any three of: tax-fund rules and caps, the foreign-income rule, withholding rates and treaties, exchange trading hours, your broker's fees, Lao tax rates and foreign-exchange rules.


## 10.8 [[10.8 Checkpoint- Phase 10 Review|Checkpoint: Phase 10 Review]]

A bond fund has a duration of about 6. Yields fall by 0.75 percentage points. Approximate price change?
?
−6 × (−0.75%) ≈ **+4.5%** (see 10.2).

A US index ETF rises 12% in dollars. USD/THB moves from 35.0 to 33.6. What's the return in baht?
?
1.12 × (33.6 ÷ 35.0) − 1 ≈ **+7.5%**: the stronger baht took away about 4.5 points (see 10.4).

Market cap 50 bn, debt 12 bn, cash 4 bn, EBITDA 7.5 bn. EV and EV/EBITDA?
?
EV = 50 + 12 − 4 = **58 bn**; EV/EBITDA = 58 ÷ 7.5 ≈ **7.7×** (see 10.6).

Northern winter. US CPI comes out at 08:30 New York time. When is that in Bangkok, and what's a 30-minute no-trade window around it?
?
**20:30** UTC+7; no new entries **20:00–21:00** (see 10.3).

Your estimate of a stock's value is 50 and you want a 25% margin of safety. Below what price would you consider buying?
?
50 × (1 − 0.25) = **37.5** (see 10.6).

Why keep the trading account and the long-term portfolio separate?
?
They have different goals, rules and time horizons. Mixing them lets trading losses (or excitement) damage the money meant for decades: never refill one from the other (see 10.7, 11.2).
