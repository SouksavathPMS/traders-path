## 8.1 [[8.1 Options Basics- Calls & Puts|Options Basics: Calls & Puts]]

Underlying::the thing the option is about (a stock like Nvidia, an index like the S&P 500, gold futures, Bitcoin).
Spot (price)::the current market price of the underlying.
Call::the right to **buy** the underlying at the strike.
Put::the right to **sell** the underlying at the strike.
Strike::the fixed price written in the contract.
Expiry (expiration)::the last day the right exists.
Premium::the price you pay for the option. The seller keeps it no matter what.
Buyer (holder / long)::pays the premium, owns the right.
Seller (writer / short)::receives the premium, must fulfil the deal if the buyer uses the right.
Exercise / assignment::the buyer *uses* the right (exercise); the seller is *made to* fulfil it (assignment).
Contract multiplier::how many units one contract controls. US stock options: **100 shares**.
Intrinsic value::what the option is worth if used right now.
Time value::the extra you pay for the *chance* things improve before expiry.
ITM / ATM / OTM::In / At / Out of the money: whether using the option now would make money (ITM), roughly break even (ATM), or not (OTM).
Implied volatility (IV)::how big a move the market *expects*, read from option prices. High IV = expensive options.
ETF::Exchange-Traded Fund: a basket of assets that trades like one stock (e.g. SPY holds the 500 S&P stocks).

## 8.2 [[8.2 The Greeks|The Greeks]]

Delta (Δ)::how much the option price moves when the underlying moves by 1. Delta 0.50 → option moves 0.50.
Gamma (Γ)::how much **delta** itself changes when the underlying moves by 1.
Theta (Θ)::how much value the option loses per day just from time passing (time decay).
Vega (ν)::how much the option price changes when implied volatility moves by 1 point.
Delta-equivalent shares::delta × 100: how many shares one contract "behaves like".
ATM / ITM / OTM::at / in / out of the money *(see 8.1)*.
0DTE::"zero days to expiry": options that expire today.
IV crush::the sharp drop in implied volatility after an event *(see 8.1)*.

## 8.3 [[8.3 Market Makers & Delta Hedging|Market Makers & Delta Hedging]]

Market maker / dealer::a firm that always quotes a buy and a sell price and earns the spread. In options, they are usually on the other side of customers.
Hedge::a trade made to reduce risk, not to profit.
Delta-neutral::a position whose value doesn't change for small price moves: total delta ≈ 0.
Underlying::the asset the options are on *(see 8.1)*.
Futures::a contract to buy or sell an asset at a set price on a future date; used by dealers to hedge quickly.
ES::the E-mini S&P 500 futures contract (Chicago Mercantile Exchange). **NQ** = E-mini Nasdaq-100.
Long gamma / short gamma::dealers who *own* options (long gamma) vs dealers who *sold* options (short gamma).
Realised volatility::how much price *actually* moved, vs implied volatility, what was *expected*.
Mean reversion::the tendency of price to come back toward an average after moving away.

## 8.4 [[8.4 Gamma Exposure (GEX) & The Gamma Flip|Gamma Exposure (GEX) & The Gamma Flip]]

GEX (gamma exposure)::estimated dollars of the underlying that dealers must trade for a 1% move.
Gamma flip / zero-gamma level::the price at which total GEX changes sign.
Open interest (OI)::number of option contracts still open at a strike *(see 8.5)*.
Contract multiplier::units per contract; 100 for US index and stock options *(see 8.1)*.
Spot (S)::current price of the underlying.
Notional::the full market value a position represents (units × price), not the money you paid.
$bn::billions of US dollars.
Regime::the market's current "mode" (calm/mean-reverting vs volatile/trending).
VIX::the S&P 500 "fear index": the implied volatility of 30-day S&P options, in % per year.
0DTE::options expiring today *(see 8.2)*.

## 8.5 [[8.5 Call Walls & Put Walls|Call Walls & Put Walls]]

Open interest (OI)::the number of option contracts that are still open (not yet closed or expired) at a strike. See the figure below.
Volume::the number of contracts traded today. High volume does **not** always mean high OI.
Call wall / put wall::the strike with the largest call (or put) OI or gamma above (or below) the price.
OPEX::options expiration day. US monthly OPEX is the **third Friday** of each month; weekly and daily (0DTE) expiries also exist.
Pin / pinning::price sticking close to a big strike into expiry.
Roll (a wall)::when traders close options at one strike and open them at another, so the wall moves.
Charm / vanna::how delta changes with time / with volatility *(see 8.6)*.

## 8.6 [[8.6 Vanna (VEX) & Charm|Vanna (VEX) & Charm]]

Delta::how much an option's price moves when the underlying moves 1 point; for puts it's negative *(see 8.2)*.
Dealer / market maker::the firm on the other side of customers' option trades, which hedges its risk *(see 8.3)*.
Hedge::an offsetting position; here, dealers short futures to balance the positive delta of the puts they're short.
Vanna::how much delta changes when implied volatility changes.
Charm::how much delta changes as time passes ("delta decay").
VEX (vanna exposure)::dealers' total vanna; like GEX *(see 8.4)*, but for volatility changes.
IV (implied volatility)::the volatility priced into options *(see 8.2)*.
IV crush::a sharp fall in IV right after an event *(see 0.6)*.
OTM / ATM (out of / at the money)::a put with strike below the current price / at it *(see 8.1)*.
OPEX (options expiration)::the day options expire; monthly OPEX is the third Friday *(see 8.5)*.
CPI (Consumer Price Index)::the main US inflation report, a big scheduled event *(see 0.8)*.
VIX::the index of expected 30-day S&P 500 volatility from option prices *(see 6.6)*.

## 8.7 [[8.7 Reading Options Flow|Reading Options Flow]]

Options flow::the stream of executed option trades, often filtered to large or unusual ones.
Print::one executed trade on the tape.
Premium::the price paid for an option; total premium = option price × 100 shares per contract × number of contracts.
Aggressor side::who crossed the spread: filled **at or above the ask** = buyer aggressive; **at or below the bid** = seller aggressive; **mid** = unclear.
Sweep::one large order split across many exchanges at once to fill fast (urgency).
Block::one large trade negotiated and printed in one piece (often institutional).
Volume::contracts traded today at that strike and expiry.
Open interest (OI)::contracts still open from previous days at that strike and expiry (updated once a day).
Opening / closing trade::creates a new position / ends an existing one.
OTM (out of the money)::a call with strike above the price, or a put below it *(see 8.1)*.
Spread (strategy)::two or more option legs traded together; one leg alone can look misleading.
Hedge::a trade that offsets another position's risk.
Unusual options activity (UOA)::paid services that alert you to large or odd prints.
