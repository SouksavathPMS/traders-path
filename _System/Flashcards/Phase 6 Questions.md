## 6.1 [[6.1 Fibonacci Retracements & Extensions|Fibonacci Retracements & Extensions]]

Is there evidence that markets "obey" the golden ratio?
?
No good evidence. Fib levels work partly because many traders watch them (self-fulfilling) and partly because pullbacks of a third to two thirds are simply common.

A down-leg runs from 80 to 60. Where is the 61.8% retracement?
?
Leg = 20. The retracement goes back **up**: 60 + 0.618 × 20 = 72.36.

Price falls through 100% of the up-leg (below its start). What does that mean?
?
The swing low broke: a CHoCH against the trend. The leg is no longer valid as a pullback in an uptrend.

A = 50, B = 60, C = 56. What is the 1.618 projection?
?
A→B = 10. 56 + 1.618 × 10 = 72.18.

Swing 200 → 260. Give the 38.2%, 50% and 61.8% retracements.
?
Leg 60. 38.2% = 260 − 22.92 = 237.08; 50% = 230; 61.8% = 260 − 37.08 = 222.92.

Why does a lone Fib level make a weak entry reason?
?
With five levels, price is almost always near one. A level only matters when it lines up with structure, a zone, an FVG or liquidity, and an LTF trigger confirms it.


## 6.2 [[6.2 Elliott Wave- The 5-3 Structure|Elliott Wave: The 5-3 Structure]]

In an uptrend, which waves move with the trend and which against it?
?
With the trend: 1, 3, 5 (motive). Against it: 2, 4 and the A-B-C correction.

Which wave is usually the longest and strongest, and why?
?
Wave 3: the majority recognises the trend, news turns positive and money floods in.

What does FOMO look like on the chart in wave 5?
?
A new high made with weaker momentum (smaller bodies, divergence), often after a sweep of buy-side liquidity, while late buyers chase.

In the wrong count, which single price check proves it wrong?
?
Wave 4's low 115.4 is below wave 1's high 120: wave 4 overlaps wave 1 (rule 3).

Name the crowd behaviour behind each of waves 1 to 5.
?
1: early/smart money buys quietly. 2: doubt, retest. 3: recognition, the crowd joins. 4: profit-taking pause. 5: late buyers, FOMO, weaker momentum.

Why should you always keep an alternative count?
?
Counts are subjective and often only clear afterwards. When the main count is invalidated, the alternative tells you what's likely happening instead of leaving you lost or hoping.


## 6.3 [[6.3 Elliott Wave Rules & Guidelines|Elliott Wave Rules & Guidelines]]

Wave 1: 50 → 58. Wave 2 ends at 49.5. Which rule breaks?
?
Rule 1: wave 2 went below wave 1's start (50). It wasn't wave 2.

Waves: 1 = 12, 3 = 9, 5 = 15. Which rule breaks?
?
Rule 2: wave 3 (9) is the shortest of the three.

Wave 2 was sharp and deep. What does alternation suggest about wave 4?
?
It's likely to be shallow and sideways (a flat or triangle), not another sharp drop.

Wave 3 is only 1.2 × wave 1 instead of 1.618. Is the count invalid?
?
No. 1.618 is a guideline. As long as wave 3 isn't the shortest and the other rules hold, the count is valid; it just scores lower.

You buy wave 2 at 105 with wave 1 running 100 → 112. Where is the rule-based stop, and what's the risk per unit?
?
Below wave 1's start, 100 (plus a buffer, e.g. 99.5). Risk ≈ 105 − 99.5 = 5.5 per unit.

State the three rules in one line each.
?
1) Wave 2 never goes below wave 1's start. 2) Wave 3 is never the shortest of 1, 3 and 5. 3) Wave 4 never overlaps wave 1's price territory (except in diagonals).

What do you do the moment a rule breaks while you're in the trade?
?
Your stop (placed at the rule level) should already have closed the trade. Then relabel with an alternative count; never move the stop to keep the original story alive.


## 6.4 [[6.4 Corrective Patterns- Zigzag, Flat, Triangle|Corrective Patterns: Zigzag, Flat, Triangle]]

A = 8 points, B ends at 92. Where does C end if C = A? If C = 1.618 × A (falling correction)?
?
C = A: 92 − 8 = 84. C = 1.618 × A: 92 − 12.94 = 79.06.

Price has made five contracting swings (A-B-C-D-E) in an uptrend. What do you wait for?
?
A close outside the triangle in the trend direction (the thrust), or a sweep of one side followed by a reversal back into the trend. Not an entry inside the triangle.

Why is wave B of a flat dangerous to short in an uptrend?
?
B often returns 90–100% of A or even beyond A's start (expanded flat), stopping out shorts; and the larger trend is still up.

Match the shape to its internal structure: zigzag, flat, triangle.
?
Zigzag 5-3-5; flat 3-3-5; triangle 3-3-3-3-3.

You're unsure which correction is forming. What's the practical approach?
?
Label it simply "correction", mark C = A / 1.618 × A and the Fib zone of the prior impulse, and wait for an LTF CHoCH in the trend direction in that zone.


## 6.5 [[6.5 Elliott + Fibonacci Confluence|Elliott + Fibonacci Confluence]]

Wave 1 runs 50 → 60. Give the 61.8% wave-2 level and the 1.618 wave-3 target from there.
?
61.8% = 60 − 6.18 = 53.82. Target = 53.82 + 16.18 = 70.0.

Why is the stop for a wave-4 long at wave 1's high and not at wave 2's low?
?
Because rule 3 says wave 4 may not overlap wave 1. Below wave 1's high, the count is already invalid, so that's where the idea is wrong.

For each of the two classic trades, name the entry zone, the rule-based stop and the target.
?
Wave 2: entry at 50–61.8% of W1; stop below W1's start; target 1.618 × W1 from the W2 low. Wave 4: entry around 38.2% of W3; stop below W1's high; target W5 = W1 from the W4 low.

W1 = 200 → 240. Calculate the 61.8% entry, the wave-3 target and the R:R with a stop at 199.
?
Entry = 240 − 24.72 = 215.28. Target = 215.28 + 64.72 = 280.0. Risk = 16.28, reward = 64.72 → ≈ 3.98R.


## 6.6 [[6.6 Standard Deviation Projections|Standard Deviation Projections]]

Mean 165 cm, SD 7 cm. Roughly what share of students are between 151 and 179 cm?
?
That's ±2 SD → about 95%.

Why can't you use the 99.7% (±3 SD) rule to say a market crash is "practically impossible"?
?
Market returns have fat tails: extreme days happen far more often than a normal distribution predicts.

VIX is 25 and the index is at 6,000. Roughly what is a 1 SD daily move in points?
?
25% ÷ 15.87 ≈ 1.58% → 6,000 × 1.58% ≈ 95 points.

Price closes above the upper Bollinger Band three days in a row during a strong uptrend. Is that a sell signal?
?
Not on its own. In strong trends price can "walk the band". A close back inside after a close outside is more meaningful, especially at a liquidity level.

The reference leg runs from 98.6 to 104.2. Where is the −1 projection, and is it a statistical level?
?
Leg = 5.6 → 104.2 + 5.6 = 109.8. It's a measured move, not statistics; use it only with liquidity or an HTF level.

Annual volatility 20%. What's the approximate daily 1 SD move in %?
?
20% ÷ √252 ≈ 20 ÷ 15.87 ≈ 1.26%.

What's the difference between a statistical standard deviation and an ICT "SD" projection?
?
Statistical SD measures how spread out returns are; ICT projections are multiples of a chosen price leg (a measured move). Only the first is statistics.


## 6.7 [[6.7 Checkpoint- Phase 6 Review|Checkpoint: Phase 6 Review]]

A BOS leg on gold runs from 1,800 to 1,960. Where are the 50% and 61.8% retracements? If the pullback ends at 1,880, where is the 1.618 extension?
?
50% = **1,880**; 61.8% = 1,960 − 0.618 × 160 = **1,861.1**. Extension = 1,880 + 1.618 × 160 = **2,138.9** (see 6.1).

Count: wave 1 runs 40 → 52, wave 2 to 45, wave 3 to 63, wave 4 to 54. Is the count valid? Where's the rule-based stop for a wave-4 long, and the W5 = W1 target?
?
Valid: W2 stays above 40, W3 (18) isn't the shortest, W4 (54) stays above W1's high (52). Stop just below **52**; target 54 + 12 = **66** (see 6.3, 6.5).

A stock at 150 has 30% annual volatility. What's the approximate 1σ move over 5 trading days, and the 2σ range?
?
σ ≈ 150 × 0.30 × √(5 ÷ 252) ≈ **6.3**. 1σ range ≈ 143.7–156.3; 2σ ≈ **137.3–162.7** (see 6.6).

State the three Elliott rules and why each one is useful as a stop.
?
W2 never beyond W1's start; W3 never the shortest of 1, 3, 5; W4 never into W1's range (except diagonals). If a rule breaks, the count is wrong, so the trade idea is wrong: that's a natural invalidation level.

What does a 2σ weekly range not tell you?
?
Direction, and the size of rare moves: returns have fat tails, so moves beyond 2σ happen more often than the normal curve suggests.
