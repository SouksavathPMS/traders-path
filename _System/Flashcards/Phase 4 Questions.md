## 4.1 [[4.1 Fear, Greed, Hope & Regret|Fear, Greed, Hope & Regret]]

Where in the emotional cycle is buying most dangerous, and where is it most rewarding?
?
Most dangerous near **euphoria** (the top, when it feels obvious). Most rewarding near **capitulation and despondency** (the bottom, when selling feels like relief).

You widen your stop because "it will come back". Which emotion is this, and what rule blocks it?
?
**Hope.** The block: a hard stop placed in the market with the entry, and only ever moved to reduce risk.

Write the three steps of "name it to tame it".
?
Rate your state 1–5 before entry; when you want to break a rule, say the emotion out loud; then ask "What does the plan say?" and do that.

Which pairs of emotions mess up entries, exits, and the trades after a loss?
?
Entries: fear and greed. Exits: fear and hope. After a loss: regret.

A system plans +3R wins at a 35% win rate. If fear makes the average win 1.8R, what's the new expectancy?
?
Planned: 0.35 × 3 − 0.65 = **+0.40R**. With 1.8R: 0.35 × 1.8 − 0.65 = **−0.02R**: slightly losing.


## 4.2 [[4.2 Cognitive Biases That Cost Money|Cognitive Biases That Cost Money]]

"It was 120 last month, so 95 is cheap." Which bias, and what rule catches it?
?
**Anchoring.** Decide on structure and current levels, not on old prices or your entry.

Your journal shows a 62% win rate, average win +0.7R and average loss −1.4R. What's happening?
?
Expectancy = 0.62 × 0.7 − 0.38 × 1.4 = 0.434 − 0.532 ≈ **−0.10R**. Classic disposition effect: winners cut, losers held.

What is a pre-mortem, and what do you do if the answer is obvious?
?
Before entry, imagine the trade has already lost and ask why. If the most likely reason is obvious (e.g. "against the HTF trend"), don't take the trade.

Why doesn't knowing about a bias protect you from it?
?
Biases work fast and automatically; careful reasoning is slow. Under pressure the fast system wins, so you need external rules, checklists and statistics.

After 3 wins in a row you want to double your size. Which bias, and what's the rule?
?
**Overconfidence** (helped by recency). The rule: fixed risk % per trade; a win streak earns nothing.


## 4.3 [[4.3 The Trading Plan & If-Then Rules|The Trading Plan & If-Then Rules]]

Why is "make 1,000 USD this month" a poor goal for your plan? Give a better one.
?
It pushes you to force trades when the market offers none. Better: a process goal you control, e.g. "follow the plan on 95% of trades and journal 100%".

Rewrite "I'll be more disciplined after losses" as an if-then rule.
?
For example: "**If** I take a loss, **then** I step away from the screen for 15 minutes and re-read the plan before the next trade."

After two losing days you want to change your stop rule, your trigger and your timeframe. What does the plan say?
?
Don't change rules mid-week. Write the ideas down; at the weekly review, change **one** rule at a time and measure it over enough trades.

Write your entry chain in one IF … AND … THEN sentence (use the lesson's as a template).
?
For example: **IF** the HTF is trending **AND** price is in an HTF zone in that direction **AND** the LTF prints a CHoCH in the HTF direction **AND** R:R ≥ 2 **AND** no circuit breaker is active **THEN** size it, place entry + stop + target, and journal it.

Account 12,000 USD, risk 0.5%, stop 950 below entry on BTC. Size?
?
60 ÷ 950 ≈ **0.063 BTC**.


## 4.4 [[4.4 Daily Routine- Before, During, After|Daily Routine: Before, During, After]]

What is the goal of each block: before, during, after?
?
Before: know your state, the day's risks and exactly what you're waiting for. During: execute the checklist only when an alert fires. After: record what happened while it's fresh and close the day.

You slept badly and rate your state 4/5. What does the routine say?
?
Trade half size or not at all. A bad day for you is a bad day to trade.

No alert has fired for two hours, and you feel you "should be doing something". What does the routine say?
?
No alert = no screen time. Close the charts; impulsive trades are born while watching "just in case".

A trade followed every rule but hit its stop. How do you grade it, and why?
?
**A**: the grade is about execution, not the result.

List three items of your before-routine.
?
Any three of: state check (1–5), economic calendar and no-trade windows, HTF state per market, zones and alerts, today's if-then plan.

It's northern winter. At what time in Bangkok does the US stock market open, and when is a 08:30 New York release?
?
Open 09:30 New York = **21:30** UTC+7; the release at 08:30 = **20:30** UTC+7.


## 4.5 [[4.5 Journaling & The Review Loop|Journaling & The Review Loop]]

A random trade with no setup made +3R. A planned trade hit its stop. Grade both.
?
Random +3R = **C** (broke the plan). Planned stop-out = **A** (followed the plan). Grade execution, not outcome.

Your weekly review suggests three changes. How many do you make next week, and why?
?
**One.** If you change several things at once, you can't tell which one helped or hurt.

Name the six groups of journal fields.
?
Plan, result, execution, mind, tags, evidence (screenshots).

A week: A-grade trades +2.4R, C-grade trades −3.0R. What's the total, and what's the conclusion?
?
Total **−0.6R**. The planned trades made money; rule-breaking cost 3.0R. Fix behaviour before changing the system.


## 4.6 [[4.6 Losing Streaks & Tilt|Losing Streaks & Tilt]]

You notice you're checking P&L every minute and skipping a checklist item. Which stage, and what's the rule?
?
**Amber.** Pause 15 minutes; the next trade at half size and A-grade only.

Why are circuit breakers decided "in Green"?
?
Because in Red your judgment is the problem. Rules made while calm, with simple triggers (number of losses, R lost), apply without needing judgment.

Your last 7 trades lost, but 5 of them were C-grade. Is the problem the system or you? What do you do?
?
Mostly you: the losses come from breaking rules. Reduce size, trade A-grade only, and work on the specific mistake tags before judging the system.

List three of your circuit-breaker triggers and their actions.
?
For example: any loss → 15-minute break; −2R or 3 losses today → done for the day; −5R this week → done for the week; −10% from peak → stop live trading and review.

Both traders in the example lost 5R in week 1. Trader B made +1.5R in week 2 at half size. What's B's two-week total, compared with A's −13R?
?
−5 + 1.5 = **−3.5R**, about 9.5R better than A, with the habits intact.


## 4.7 [[4.7 Checkpoint- Phase 4 Review|Checkpoint: Phase 4 Review]]

The plan: 45% wins at +2R, losses −1R. Fear makes you close 40% of winners at +0.5R. New average win and expectancy?
?
Average win = 0.6 × 2 + 0.4 × 0.5 = **1.4R**. Expectancy = 0.45 × 1.4 − 0.55 = **+0.08R**, down from +0.35R (see 4.1).

Planned EUR/USD long: entry 1.0850, stop 1.0820, target 1.0940. You miss it and chase at 1.0870. R:R before and after, and the new break-even win rate?
?
Planned: 90 ÷ 30 = **3.0**. Chased: 70 ÷ 50 = **1.4** → break-even 1 ÷ 2.4 ≈ **42%** (was 25%) (see 4.3).

A week: 5 A-grade trades = +2.0R, 2 C-grade trades = −2.5R. Total, and what to work on?
?
Total **−0.5R**. The plan made +2.0R; the two rule-breaks cost 2.5R. Work on the C-trade tags, not the system (see 4.5).

Northern winter. When is an FOMC decision (14:00 New York) in UTC+7, and what's a 30-minute no-trade window around it?
?
**02:00** UTC+7; no new entries **01:30–02:30** (see 4.4, 10.3).

Name two personal Amber signs and the rule that applies at Amber.
?
For example: checking P&L every minute, skipping a checklist item, clicking faster, annoyance at the market. Rule: pause 15 minutes; next trade half size, A-grade only.

When are you allowed to change your plan's rules?
?
Only at scheduled reviews (weekly/monthly), one rule at a time, measured over enough trades. Never mid-week or after a single loss.
