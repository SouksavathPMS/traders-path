## 12.1 [[12.1 Black Monday 1987|Black Monday 1987]]

Black Monday::Monday 19 October 1987, the largest one-day percentage fall in US stock history.
S&P 500 / Dow::the two main US stock indices *(see 0.5)*.
Portfolio insurance::a strategy that sold index futures automatically as prices fell, to limit losses.
Program trading::computer-driven orders that buy or sell many stocks at once *(see 9.4)*.
Liquidity::buyers and sellers available to trade with *(see 1.2)*.
Gap / fast market::price jumping past levels without trading at them, so stops fill far away *(see 0.5)*.
50-day average::the average of the last 50 daily closes, used as a trend filter *(see 2.8)*.
Leverage::trading a position bigger than your account using borrowed money *(see 0.2)*.
Lender of last resort::the central bank promising to lend to keep the financial system working *(see 10.2)*.

## 12.2 [[12.2 The 2008 Financial Crisis|The 2008 Financial Crisis]]

Subprime mortgage::a home loan to a borrower with a weak credit record.
Securitisation::packaging many loans into a bond that investors can buy.
Leverage::using borrowed money; banks with high leverage lose their capital quickly when assets fall *(see 0.2)*.
Bankruptcy (Chapter 11)::the legal process when a company can't pay its debts.
Money market fund "breaking the buck"::a fund meant to be as safe as cash falling below 1 USD per share.
TARP::the US government's 700-billion-dollar bank rescue programme (2008).
Bear market::a fall of 20% or more from a peak.
40-week average::the average of the last 40 weekly closes (about 200 trading days), a common long-term trend filter *(see 2.8)*.
Counterparty risk::the risk that the firm on the other side of your trade or account fails *(see 0.1)*.

## 12.3 [[12.3 The Swiss Franc Shock (2015)|The Swiss Franc Shock (2015)]]

SNB::the Swiss National Bank, Switzerland's central bank.
CHF::the Swiss franc.
Floor (minimum exchange rate)::a level a central bank promises to defend by buying or selling currency.
Peg::a currency held at or near a fixed rate *(see 0.3)*.
Negative interest rate::a central bank charging banks to hold deposits.
Gap risk::the risk that price jumps past your stop *(see 10.3)*.
Negative balance::losing more than your account holds, so you owe the broker *(see 0.2)*.
Negative-balance protection::a rule that your account can't go below zero.
ECB reference rate::the daily exchange rate published by the European Central Bank.

## 12.4 [[12.4 The COVID Crash (2020)|The COVID Crash (2020)]]

Pandemic::a disease spreading across many countries.
Lockdown::government orders closing businesses and limiting movement.
Circuit breaker (market-wide)::an automatic trading pause when the S&P 500 falls 7%, 13% or 20% in a day.
Volatility::how much prices swing *(see 6.6)*.
Quantitative easing (QE)::the central bank buying bonds to support markets *(see 10.2)*.
Drawdown rules::pre-set actions at set losses (halve risk, stop and review) *(see 3.6)*.
Rebalancing::trading back to target weights *(see 10.7, 11.2)*.
V-shaped recovery::a fall followed by an equally fast rise.

## 12.5 [[12.5 The 2022 Rate Shock|The 2022 Rate Shock]]

Inflation::prices rising across the economy *(see 10.1)*.
Federal funds rate::the US central bank's policy rate *(see 10.2)*.
Rate hike::raising the policy rate; 0.75 percentage points = "75 basis points".
Bond yield::the yearly return a bond pays relative to its price; prices move opposite to yields.
Duration::roughly how much a bond's price falls (in %) when yields rise by one percentage point *(see 10.2)*.
60/40 portfolio::60% stocks, 40% bonds *(see 10.7)*.
Correlation regime::the way markets move together, which can change *(see 10.4)*.
Whipsaw::a trend signal that reverses quickly, causing a series of small losses.

## 12.6 [[12.6 The FTX Collapse (2022)|The FTX Collapse (2022)]]

Exchange (crypto)::a company where you buy, sell and often store crypto *(see 0.7)*.
Custody::who actually controls the assets; "not your keys, not your coins" *(see 0.7)*.
Exchange token (FTT)::a token issued by the exchange itself, whose value depends on the exchange.
Alameda Research::the trading firm owned by FTX's founder, closely linked to FTX.
Bank run::many customers withdrawing at once because they fear they won't get their money *(see 12.7)*.
Chapter 11 bankruptcy::a US court process to reorganise or wind up a company.
Creditor / claim::someone owed money / their right to be repaid.
Petition date::the day of the bankruptcy filing; claims were valued in dollars at that date.
Counterparty risk::the risk the firm holding your money fails *(see 0.1)*.

## 12.7 [[12.7 Silicon Valley Bank (2023)|Silicon Valley Bank (2023)]]

Bank run::many depositors withdrawing at once because they fear the bank will fail.
Deposit insurance::a government guarantee on deposits up to a limit (US FDIC 250,000 USD; Thailand DPA 1,000,000 THB per depositor per bank).
Uninsured deposits::money above the insurance limit.
Held-to-maturity (HTM) bonds::bonds a bank plans to keep until they repay; their price losses don't show in profits unless they're sold.
Unrealised loss::a loss on paper that becomes real only when you sell.
Duration::how sensitive a bond's price is to interest rates *(see 10.2, 12.5)*.
FDIC receivership::the US deposit insurer taking control of a failed bank.
BTFP (Bank Term Funding Program)::a Fed programme letting banks borrow against bonds at their face value.
Contagion::fear spreading from one institution to similar ones.
