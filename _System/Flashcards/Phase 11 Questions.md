## 11.1 [[11.1 Write Your Trading Plan|Write Your Trading Plan]]

Which phases are required in every plan, and how should you add an optional module?
?
Required: structure (2), risk (3), psychology & execution (4), measurement (9). Add one optional module at a time and keep it only if your journal shows it improves expectancy.

Is "trade with the trend and use good risk management" an acceptable section 3/6? Rewrite it.
?
No, it's too vague. For example: "Bias: daily HH + HL = longs only; range = no trades. Risk: 0.5% per trade, max 1.5% open, size = 1R ÷ (stop + costs)."

You're in micro live at 0.25%. After 50 trades, expectancy is −0.15R and you broke circuit breakers twice. What does the plan say?
?
Step back a stage (to paper trading): negative expectancy over 50 trades and repeated circuit-breaker violations are both step-back triggers.

Name the four stages from plan to live money and one gate for each.
?
Backtest (50+ occurrences), paper trade (30+ real-time trades with the full routine), micro live at 0.25% (50+ trades), normal size 0.5–1% (only after the plan has held up and you follow it reliably).

Account 20,000 USD, risk 0.5%, gold stop 6 USD per oz plus 0.4 USD costs, 1 lot = 100 oz. Size?
?
1R = **100 USD**. 100 ÷ (6.4 × 100) = 0.156 → **0.15 lot** (risk 96 USD).


## 11.2 [[11.2 Write Your Investment Policy|Write Your Investment Policy]]

Which section answers "what do I do if my portfolio falls 30%?", and why must it be written before a crash?
?
The **crisis plan**. During a crash, fear makes selling feel urgent and right (4.1); a pre-written plan turns the moment into a checklist.

Your trading account lost 25% this year. Can you move money from the IPS portfolio to refill it?
?
No. Never refill the trading account from the IPS after losses, and never raid the IPS to "make back" trading losses. Write this rule into both documents.

Name the eight sections of an IPS.
?
Goals & horizon, risk tolerance, target allocation, instruments & costs, contributions, rebalancing rule, crisis plan, review.

Target: stocks 70% ± 5, bonds 30%. After a rally, the portfolio is 600,000 THB stocks and 200,000 THB bonds. Is a band broken, and what's the rebalance trade?
?
Stocks = 600 ÷ 800 = **75%**, at the top of the band (≤ 75%), so not yet broken. At a calendar rebalance: target stocks 560,000 → **sell 40,000** stocks, buy 40,000 bonds.


## 11.3 [[11.3 The Lifelong Learning Loop|The Lifelong Learning Loop]]

What is the output of a weekly review, and of a monthly review?
?
Weekly: **one** improvement to test. Monthly: a plan version update (v1.1, v1.2 …) based on expectancy per setup, drawdown and comparison with buy & hold.

A YouTube strategy claims 80% wins. What are the five steps before it enters your plan?
?
Write the rule precisely, backtest it on 50+ occurrences, paper trade it alongside your plan (tagged separately), compare expectancy, drawdown and trade count with your current setup, and keep it only if it improves results with enough trades.

Your setup has lost for six weeks. Which lessons do you go back to first, and what question do they answer?
?
9.1–9.3: is this variance or has something changed? Then 2.2 and 7.1: what's the current regime? Also check your journal grades (4.5) to rule out execution problems.

Name the five review cadences and what each produces.
?
Daily: clean data. Weekly: one improvement to test. Monthly: a plan version update. Quarterly: a keep/drop decision on a new module. Yearly: an updated plan and IPS.

What does a changelog entry need, and why?
?
A date, the change, the reason and the data behind it. It turns your plan's history into evidence, and lets you undo changes that didn't help.
