## 0.1 [[0.1 Why Exchanges & Brokers Exist|Why Exchanges & Brokers Exist]]

Your broker goes bankrupt. In which model are your assets most likely safe?
?
The exchange model: your shares are held by a custodian in your name, separate from the broker. In the CFD model you only have a contract with the broker, so you depend on segregated accounts and the regulator's compensation scheme.

Why might a market-maker broker not like a trader who wins consistently?
?
Because if it keeps your trades on its own book, your profit is its loss. Good brokers hedge such clients; bad ones widen spreads, slip orders or delay withdrawals.

In one sentence each: what does an exchange do, and what does a broker do?
?
An exchange is the regulated marketplace that matches buyers and sellers at one public price; a broker is the licensed company that gives you access to that market (or fills your orders itself) and holds your account.


## 0.2 [[0.2 Instruments & Leverage- Spot, CFD, Futures, ETF|Instruments & Leverage: Spot, CFD, Futures, ETF]]

You buy gold through a CFD and your broker goes bankrupt. Do you own any gold?
?
No. A CFD is only a contract with the broker. You have a claim on the broker for your account balance, protected only by segregated accounts and your regulator's rules.

Which two wrappers have an expiry date?
?
Futures and options. Spot, CFDs and ETFs can be held with no end date (CFDs charge swap every night instead).

Same account and trade, but you buy only **0.02 lot** (2 oz). How far must gold fall before the stop out at 50%?
?
Margin = 2 × 4,000 ÷ 100 = 80 USD. Stop out when equity = 40 → loss 1,960 → 1,960 ÷ 2 = 980 USD drop (gold at 3,020). With a sensible size, the stop out is so far away that your own stop loss always comes first.

What is the difference between a margin call and a stop out?
?
A margin call is a warning that you are close to the limit (add money or reduce size). A stop out is the broker actually closing your positions, usually starting with the biggest loser.

You hold 1 MGC (Micro Gold) contract and gold rises from 4,000 to 4,025. What is your profit?
?
25 USD × 10 oz = 250 USD (before commission).

Your chart shows a sudden 30 USD jump on gold futures on a quiet day with no news. What might it be?
?
A roll in a continuous contract: the chart switched from the expiring month to the next month, which trades higher in contango. It isn't a real move.

Your gold CFD swap is −6.58 USD per night and the broker charges triple swap on Wednesday. You hold from Monday to Friday (4 nights). How much swap do you pay?
?
Monday, Tuesday and Thursday nights = 3 × 6.58; Wednesday = 3 × 6.58. Total = 6 nights' worth = 39.48 USD.

Why is the swap on a long gold CFD usually negative?
?
Gold pays no interest, but the broker is effectively lending you the money to hold it. You pay that interest (plus markup) every night.

An index goes +5% and then −5%. Where does a 2× ETF end, starting from 100?
?
Day 1: +10% → 110. Day 2: −10% → 99. The 2× ETF is down 1% while the index is at 100 × 1.05 × 0.95 = 99.75 (−0.25%).

Name the five wrappers from this lesson and, for each one, who is on the other side of your trade.
?
Spot: the seller. CFD: your broker. Futures: the exchange's clearing house. ETF: other investors on the stock exchange (you own fund shares). Option: the option seller, guaranteed by the clearing house.

An account of 2,000 USD buys 0.10 lot of gold (10 oz) at 4,000 with 1:100 leverage. What is the margin, and how many USD does the account lose for each 1 USD that gold falls?
?
Position value 40,000 → margin 400 USD. Each 1 USD drop = −10 USD.


## 0.3 [[0.3 Forex- How Currencies Trade|Forex: How Currencies Trade]]

USD/JPY moves from 150.00 to 151.00. Did the dollar get stronger or weaker? How many pips?
?
Stronger: one dollar now buys more yen. For JPY pairs 1 pip = 0.01, so the move is 100 pips.

Same account and 1% risk, but the chart needs a 50-pip stop. What lot size?
?
Pip value = 10 ÷ 50 = 0.20 USD → 0.20 ÷ 10 = 0.02 lots. A wider stop means a smaller position, so the money at risk stays the same.

Account 5,000 USD, risk 1%, stop 25 pips on GBP/USD. What lot size? If the broker offers 1:500 leverage instead of 1:100, does your risk change?
?
Risk 50 USD ÷ 25 pips = 2 USD/pip → 0.20 lots. No: risk is set by lot size × stop distance. Leverage only changes how much margin is locked.


## 0.4 [[0.4 Gold (XAU-USD)|Gold (XAU/USD)]]

World gold is flat today, but the Thai gold price rose by 190 THB. What probably happened?
?
The baht weakened against the dollar (USD/THB went up by about 0.10). Each dollar of gold now costs more baht.

Why does gold compete with interest rates?
?
Gold pays nothing. Money in a safe bond earns interest. The more a bond pays after inflation, the more you give up by holding gold instead.

Account 5,000 USD, risk 1%, stop 25 USD. How many lots (1 lot = 100 oz)?
?
Risk = 50 USD. Per lot = 25 × 100 = 2,500. 50 ÷ 2,500 = 0.02 lots.

Your broker's spec says 1 lot = 10 oz. You copy the size from the walkthrough (0.04 lots) with a 5 USD stop. How much do you risk?
?
0.04 × 10 oz = 0.4 oz × 5 USD = 2 USD, ten times less than planned. On a broker where 1 lot = 100 oz the same number of lots risks 20 USD. Always check the spec.

US inflation comes in **lower** than forecast. What is the typical first reaction in yields, the dollar and gold?
?
Rate cuts look more likely → yields fall → the dollar weakens → gold tends to rise.

Why might gold rise on a day when US yields also rise?
?
Another driver is stronger that day: for example a war headline (fear), or news of large central-bank buying.

In January (northern winter), at what time UTC+7 is the LBMA PM gold auction?
?
15:00 London (GMT = UTC+0) → 22:00 UTC+7.

Why are stops of 2–3 USD on gold usually a bad idea?
?
Gold's normal daily movement is tens of USD. A 2–3 USD stop sits inside normal noise and the spread, so it gets hit by random movement, not because the idea was wrong.

Gold drops to 3,991, then closes the candle back at 4,006. What might have happened at 4,000?
?
A sweep: sell stops under the round number were triggered, big buyers filled their orders against them, and price returned above the level. Wait for the close back inside before acting on it (see 5.2).

Name the four main drivers of gold and the usual direction of each.
?
Real interest rates (up → gold down), US dollar (up → gold down), fear and crisis (up → gold up), central-bank buying (up → gold up).

Account 2,000 USD, risk 1%, stop 10 USD, 1 lot = 100 oz. What size, and what is the loss if the stop is hit?
?
20 ÷ (10 × 100) = 0.02 lots (2 oz). Loss = 2 × 10 = 20 USD.

What two prices do you need to estimate the Thai gold bar price?
?
XAU/USD and USD/THB. Thai price ≈ XAU/USD × USD/THB × 0.47295.


## 0.5 [[0.5 Stocks & Indices|Stocks & Indices]]

You buy 50 shares of a company on the stock exchange today. Does the company receive your money?
?
No. You bought on the secondary market from another investor. The company only receives money when it issues new shares (IPO or a later share sale).

A stock is split 4-for-1. You had 10 shares at 400 USD. What do you have now?
?
40 shares at 100 USD: still 4,000 USD.

A company reports profit up 30%, but the stock falls 8%. How is that possible?
?
Analysts expected more (say +40%), or the guidance for next quarter was weak. Prices move on the surprise against expectations, not on whether the number is "good".

Rates rise sharply. Which is likely to fall more: a utility paying a steady dividend, or a young software company with little profit today?
?
Usually the young software company, because most of its value is in profits far in the future, which are discounted more heavily when rates are high.

In a market-cap weighted index, which matters more: a 5% move in a company worth 4 trillion USD, or a 20% move in a company worth 50 billion?
?
The 4-trillion company: 5% of 4,000 bn = 200 bn of value; 20% of 50 bn = 10 bn. The big company's move is 20 times larger in index terms.

It is January. At what time UTC+7 does the US regular session open?
?
21:30 (northern winter; 20:30 in summer).

Why can't a stop loss fully protect you over an earnings report?
?
Results come out when the market is closed. The next open can be far beyond your stop, and a stop becomes a market order that fills at the first available price.

A stock falls exactly by its dividend amount at the open, on a quiet day. What is the most likely reason?
?
It's the ex-dividend date: new buyers no longer get the dividend, so the price adjusts down by about that amount.

Economists start predicting a recession. Which group of sectors do investors usually move into?
?
Defensives: consumer staples, healthcare and utilities, because demand for their products drops less in a downturn.

In one sentence: what does a share give you, and what moves its price?
?
A share is a small piece of ownership of a business's future profits; its price moves when the market's expectations of those profits (or the interest rate used to value them) change.

Why can a few companies move the whole S&P 500?
?
It is market-cap weighted, and the biggest companies are huge: the top 10 made up about 39% of the index in late September 2026.

Price 60 USD, EPS 3 USD. What is the P/E, and what does it mean?
?
60 ÷ 3 = 20: you pay 20 years of today's profit for the share, and the market expects at least steady profits ahead.


## 0.6 [[0.6 Case Study- Nvidia|Case Study: Nvidia]]

A hyperscaler announces it will cut next year's data-centre spending by 20%. Why might NVDA fall on that news, even though Nvidia itself said nothing?
?
Because hyperscaler capex is Nvidia's main source of revenue. Less spending by a big customer means lower expected future sales, so the market lowers its guess of Nvidia's future profits.

Nvidia's results are "great", but the stock falls 5% the next day. Give two possible reasons.
?
(1) The results or guidance were below the very high expectations (the whisper number), even if above consensus. (2) The options market had priced an even bigger move, and many traders who bought before the report sold afterwards ("sell the news", see 0.8). Other answers: worries in the call about China, margins or competition.

A 300 USD stock has an at-the-money straddle priced at 24 USD before earnings. What is the implied move, and what range is "expected"?
?
24 ÷ 300 = 8% → about 276 to 324.

You buy that straddle and the stock moves 3%. Why do you probably lose money?
?
The move is smaller than the 8% priced in, and IV crush after the report removes most of the remaining time value.

NVDA is 8% of the S&P 500 and falls 10%. All other stocks are flat. What happens to the index?
?
About 0.08 × (−10%) = −0.8%.

Why are NVDA options much more expensive the day before earnings than a week after?
?
Before the report, a big move is expected (high IV). After the report the uncertainty is gone, IV falls and the options lose that extra value (IV crush).

Why can Nvidia's earnings move the S&P 500?
?
The index is market-cap weighted and Nvidia is its largest weight (about 8.3% in late Sep 2026). An 8–9% move in NVDA alone shifts the index by roughly 0.7 percentage points.

What does an implied move of 6% before earnings tell you, and what doesn't it tell you?
?
It tells you the options market expects a move of roughly ±6%. It does not tell you the direction, and option buyers only profit if the actual move is bigger than that.

Name three things other than Nvidia's own results that can move NVDA.
?
Hyperscaler capex plans, export rules to China, competition (AMD, custom chips, cheaper AI), TSMC supply, interest rates and overall market mood.


## 0.7 [[0.7 Crypto- Bitcoin, Ether & Stablecoins|Crypto: Bitcoin, Ether & Stablecoins]]

Why is it so hard to change a past Bitcoin transaction?
?
Because thousands of computers hold identical copies of the ledger and each block is sealed to the one before it. You'd have to rewrite most copies at once, which costs more than it's worth.

USDT trades at 0.97 USD on a day of bad news. What is the market worried about?
?
That the issuer's reserves may not be enough (or accessible) to redeem every coin at 1 USD.

You buy bitcoin on an exchange and leave it there. Who controls it?
?
The exchange holds the private keys. You own a claim on the exchange. If it fails or freezes withdrawals, you may not get your coins back (or only years later).

What is the one thing you must never type into a website or tell anyone?
?
Your seed phrase (or private key). Anyone who has it can take all your coins, and no one can reverse it.

You're in Bangkok. At what local time does a typical crypto daily candle close?
?
07:00 UTC+7 (00:00 UTC), all year.

Funding on a Bitcoin perp has been very positive for a week. What does that tell you?
?
Longs are crowded and paying a lot to stay in. If price drops, many leveraged longs can be liquidated together (a long squeeze).

With 1,000 USD margin at 20× on Bitcoin at 80,000, roughly how far can price fall before liquidation (same 0.5% rule)?
?
Position 20,000 USD = 0.25 BTC. Maintenance 100 USD → loss 900 → 900 ÷ 0.25 = 3,600 USD → about 76,400 (−4.5%).

The Fed surprises with a rate cut. What is the usual first reaction in Bitcoin?
?
Usually up: easier money and a weaker dollar tend to support risk assets. It's a tendency, not a rule (see "priced in", 0.8).

What does "not your keys, not your coins" mean?
?
If someone else (an exchange) holds the private keys, you only have a promise from them. If they fail or freeze withdrawals, you may lose access to your coins.

Why are liquidation cascades common in crypto?
?
Many traders use high leverage, and their liquidation prices cluster at similar levels. When price reaches one cluster, forced selling pushes it into the next.

Name two ways a stablecoin can fail to be worth 1 USD.
?
Reserves that are lost, missing or not liquid enough, and a run where everyone tries to redeem at once (or the issuer freezes redemptions).


## 0.8 [[0.8 What Moves Markets- News & the Economic Calendar|What Moves Markets: News & the Economic Calendar]]

US CPI forecast 3.3%, actual 3.1%. What is the typical first reaction for gold?
?
Gold often rises: lower-than-expected inflation makes rate cuts more likely, which weakens the dollar and lowers real yields, both good for gold.

CPI comes in exactly at forecast. Why might the market still move a lot?
?
Because of the details (core vs headline), revisions, or because traders were positioned for a surprise and now unwind. "In line" removes uncertainty, which can itself trigger a move.

It's 19:20 on NFP Friday. You see a perfect setup on EUR/USD on the 5-minute chart. What do you do?
?
Nothing yet. NFP is at 19:30: spreads will widen and price can spike through any stop. Wait until about 19:45, then re-assess the setup on the new structure.


## 0.9 [[0.9 Staying Safe- Scams, Fake Brokers & Signal Groups|Staying Safe: Scams, Fake Brokers & Signal Groups]]

A platform says you must pay a fee before you can withdraw your profits. What should you do?
?
Stop sending money. Real brokers subtract fees from your balance; a demand for new money before withdrawal is a classic scam sign. Collect evidence and report it (section 5).

Why do scammers let you withdraw a small amount at first?
?
To build trust so you deposit much more. The small withdrawal is part of the bait.

A group promises "a stable 5% every month from AI arbitrage" and pays a bonus for every friend who joins. Name two red flags.
?
A fixed, guaranteed monthly return, and rewards for recruiting. (Also: a vague, impressive-sounding strategy.)

A broker's website shows a licence number from the UK FCA. Is that enough?
?
No. Look the number up on the FCA register yourself and check that the firm name, website and phone number listed there match exactly. Clone firms copy real licence numbers.

A signal group shows 30 winning trades in its channel. Why is this not evidence of skill?
?
You don't know how many losing trades were never posted or deleted, or how many other groups failed. Only a complete, verified record over a long period means anything.

Name four red flags of an investment scam.
?
Any four of: guaranteed or fixed returns; urgency; secrecy; rewards for recruiting; no licence on the regulator's register; problems or fees when withdrawing; celebrity or "mentor" lifestyle ads; they contacted you first.

You were scammed. A company messages you offering to recover the money for a 15% upfront fee. What do you do?
?
Don't pay. It's almost certainly a recovery scam. Report the original scam to your bank, the police (1441 in Thailand) and the regulator, and keep your evidence.

Why do signal groups and mentors seem so successful online?
?
Survivorship bias and selective posting: you only see winners and winning trades. Many also earn from course fees and broker referrals, not from trading.


## 0.11 [[0.11 Personal Finance First- Emergency Fund, Debt & Money You Can Afford to Risk|Personal Finance First: Emergency Fund, Debt & Money You Can Afford to Risk]]

Why does paying off a credit card come before investing?
?
Repaying a 16% card is a guaranteed 16% a year "return"; very few investments reliably beat that, and none without risk.

Where in the ladder does trading belong, and with what money?
?
Last, and only with risk capital: money you could lose completely without changing your life.

Your essentials are 24,000 THB a month and your income is commission-based. How big should the full emergency fund be?
?
Irregular income → towards 6 months: 6 × 24,000 = **144,000 THB**.

Is it OK to keep the emergency fund in a crypto exchange because "it's easy to withdraw"?
?
No. It must not lose value when you need it, and a crypto balance can fall 50% or be frozen. Keep it in a bank savings account.

You owe 30,000 THB on a card at 16% and have 30,000 THB you planned to use for trading. What's the better move?
?
Pay off the card: a guaranteed 16% a year saved (about 4,800 THB). Start trading later, with risk capital.

Why is paying only the minimum each month so expensive?
?
Most of each small payment goes to interest, so the balance falls very slowly and interest keeps compounding for years.

Savings 250,000 THB, essentials 15,000 THB a month, nothing needed in the next 3 years. Using a 6-month fund and a 10% cap, what's the trading money?
?
Fund 90,000 → investable 160,000 → trading money ≤ **16,000 THB**.

Give the one-line test for risk capital.
?
"If I lost all of it, would my life change?" If yes, it isn't risk capital.

Put these in order: trading, full emergency fund, budget, pay off a 16% card, starter emergency fund, long-term investing.
?
Budget → starter emergency fund → pay off the 16% card → full emergency fund → long-term investing → trading.

Investable money is 200,000 THB. What's the largest trading account by this lesson's rule, and what's 1R at 1% risk?
?
≤ 10% → **20,000 THB**; 1R = **200 THB**.


## 0.12 [[0.12 Short Selling- How It Works, Borrow Fees & Short Squeezes|Short Selling: How It Works, Borrow Fees & Short Squeezes]]

You short 200 shares at 30 and cover at 33. Result before costs?
?
(30 − 33) × 200 = **−600**.

Why do you owe the dividend when you're short?
?
The lender still owns the economic rights. The buyer of the shares you sold receives the dividend, so you must pay the same amount to the lender.

Position 10,000, borrow fee 12% a year, held 15 days. Fee?
?
10,000 × 0.12 × 15 ÷ 360 = **50**.

Short interest 6 million shares, average volume 1.5 million a day. Days to cover?
?
6 ÷ 1.5 = **4 days**.

Why can a stop on a short fill far above its level during a squeeze?
?
A squeeze often gaps up or moves in big jumps; the stop becomes a market buy that fills at the next available price.

You expect EUR/USD to fall. Do you need to borrow anything?
?
No. Selling EUR/USD (a CFD or spot position) is just a short position; you pay or receive swap overnight, but there's no share loan.

Name four costs or risks a stock short has that a long doesn't.
?
Borrow fee, paying dividends, recall risk, unlimited loss / squeeze risk (and rules like the uptick rule).

You short 50 shares at 120 with a stop at 126. Price gaps to 135 at the open. Loss?
?
(120 − 135) × 50 = **−750**, not the planned −300: the stop fills at the first available price.


## 0.13 [[0.13 TradingView Basics- Charts, Drawing Tools, Indicators & Alerts|TradingView Basics: Charts, Drawing Tools, Indicators & Alerts]]

Your watchlist shows `PTT  D  +0.50%`. What does the D tell you?
?
The data is **delayed** (often by 15 minutes or more), so the price you see is not the price you can trade right now. Use your broker's live quote for entries.

Why does it matter whether you open `XAUUSD` or `GC1!`?
?
They are different instruments: a spot/CFD gold price from a broker vs the front-month gold futures contract. Their prices, trading hours and contract sizes differ *(see 0.2, 0.4)*. Analyse the one you actually trade.

Entry 1.2700, stop 1.2670, target 1.2790 on GBP/USD. Risk:reward?
?
Risk = 30 pips, reward = 90 pips → **1 : 3.0**.

You risk 50 USD with a 25-pip stop on EUR/USD (1 pip = 10 USD per lot). Size?
?
50 ÷ (25 × 10) = **0.20 lots**.

A stock went from 5 to 50 over 15 years. Which scale shows whether its yearly growth was steady?
?
**Log scale**: steady percentage growth appears as a straight line on a log scale, but as a curve bending upward on a linear scale.

On the free plan you want a 1-minute chart of EUR/USD going back two weeks. Will 5,000 bars be enough?
?
No. 5,000 one-minute bars cover only about **3.5 days** of 24-hour forex trading. Use a 5-minute chart (about 17 days) or a higher timeframe.

Why put your plan in the alert message?
?
So that when the alert fires, you read your own rules first instead of reacting to the price move. It turns the alert into a checklist.

Name the seven parts of a charting screen.
?
Symbol search, timeframes, drawing tools, indicators, alerts, price scale, data status (delayed or real-time).

Entry 2,400, stop 2,388, target 2,436 on a gold chart. Risk:reward?
?
Risk 12, reward 36 → **1 : 3.0**.

Why are alerts more useful than a third indicator?
?
An alert removes the need to watch the screen and brings your written plan to you at the right moment; another indicator usually repeats information you already have.


## 0.14 [[0.14 Economic Calendars & Stock Screeners in Practice|Economic Calendars & Stock Screeners in Practice]]

NFP forecast +150K, actual +95K, previous revised from +180K to +120K. Is this a strong or weak report?
?
Weak. The new number missed by 55K and the previous month was revised down by 60K; both point to a slowing job market.

Two calendar sites show different forecasts for the same CPI release. Which one is "right"?
?
Neither exactly: each is an average of a different group of economists. The difference is usually small; what matters is that the actual number comes from the official agency (here the BLS).

US CPI is released on Thursday 10 December 2026 at 08:30 New York time. What time is that in Bangkok?
?
**20:30** (the US is on winter time, UTC−5, so the gap is 12 hours).

Why is the week after the last Sunday of October tricky for session traders in Asia?
?
Europe has already moved its clocks back but the US has not, so London opens one hour later for us (15:00) while New York still opens at 20:30. The usual gap between the two sessions changes for that week.

Why put size and liquidity filters first?
?
They protect you: tiny, thinly traded stocks have wide spreads and can be hard to sell, and they are favourites for pump-and-dump schemes *(see 0.9)*.

A screen for "dividend yield above 9%" returns a stock whose price fell 60% this year. What should you suspect?
?
A **yield trap**: the yield is high because the price collapsed, and the dividend may be cut *(see 10.12)*.

What three things must you know before a top-importance release?
?
The time (in UTC+7), the forecast, and whether your open positions and stops can survive a spike.

FOMC decision on Wednesday 9 December 2026, 14:00 New York. Bangkok time?
?
**Thursday 10 December, 02:00**.

A screener gives you 12 stocks. What's the next step?
?
Study them by hand: chart structure, the business and its numbers, upcoming earnings and ex-dividend dates. Then trade only if one fits your plan.


## 0.15 [[0.15 Prop Firms & Funded Challenges- How They Work and the Real Odds|Prop Firms & Funded Challenges: How They Work and the Real Odds]]

In FTMO's model, what kind of account do you trade after passing?
?
A **demo (simulated) account**. FTMO states all accounts are demo accounts with fictitious funds; payouts are real money paid from the firm's revenue.

Per the FPFX data, of 100 challenge buyers, how many received any payout?
?
About **7**.

Static 10% maximum loss on 100,000, daily 5% of initial capital. The day starts at 96,000. What is today's floor?
?
Daily floor = 96,000 − 5,000 = 91,000; static floor = 90,000. The higher one applies first: **91,000**.

Trailing 2,000 limit on 50,000. End-of-day balances: 50,800, then 50,300. Floor after day 2?
?
After day 1: 50,800 − 2,000 = **48,800**. Day 2 is lower, so the floor stays at **48,800**.

Why does a coin-flip trader pass phase 1 about half the time?
?
With equal +10% target and −10% limit and no edge, the account is a random walk that is equally likely to hit either barrier first (the daily limit lowers this at larger sizes).

A trader with a real edge goes from 1% to 3% risk "to pass faster". What happens to the pass rate in the simulation?
?
It falls from **96.9%** to **49.3%**, because a few losses in one day now break the 5% daily limit.

Where does most of a challenge-based prop firm's revenue come from?
?
From **challenge fees**, most of which are paid by traders who fail.

Day starts at 102,000 on a 100,000 account with a 5% daily limit on initial capital. Floor?
?
102,000 − 5,000 = **97,000**.

Name two reasons passing a challenge is weak evidence of skill.
?
Luck: a zero-edge trader passes phase 1 about half the time; and the sample is small (a few dozen trades). Bigger risk can also raise a losing trader's pass chance.


## 0.10 [[0.10 Checkpoint- Phase 0 Review|Checkpoint: Phase 0 Review]]

Gold is at 4,000, your account is 2,000 USD, you risk 1% and your stop is 10 USD away. 1 lot = 100 oz. What size?
?
20 ÷ (10 × 100) = 0.02 lots (2 oz). If the stop is hit: 2 × 10 = 20 USD.

Why can a stop loss fail to protect you on an earnings night or a weekend?
?
The market is closed (or very thin) when the news arrives. Price can open far beyond your stop, and the stop fills at the first available price (see 0.2, 0.5).
