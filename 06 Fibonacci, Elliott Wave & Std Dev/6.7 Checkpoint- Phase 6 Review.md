# Checkpoint: Phase 6 Review

> [!mindset]
> Phase 6 gave you three rulers: **Fibonacci** (proportion), **Elliott** (rhythm) and **standard deviation** (normal range). None of them predicts. All of them help you place entries, stops and targets in sensible places, as long as structure, liquidity and risk agree.

![[p6-summary.en.svg]]

## Key takeaways

| Lesson | The one thing to remember |
|---|---|
| 6.1 Fibonacci | Retrace 38.2/50/61.8/78.6 from the BOS leg; project 1.0/1.272/1.618. Only with confluence. |
| 6.2 Elliott 5-3 | Five waves with the trend, three against; fractal; wave 3 usually strongest. |
| 6.3 Rules | W2 never beyond W1's start · W3 never shortest · W4 never into W1. Rules = stops. |
| 6.4 Corrections | Zigzag 5-3-5 · flat 3-3-5 · triangle. Corrections are messy: wait for the end. |
| 6.5 Confluence | Buy W2 at 50–61.8% → target 1.618; buy W4 at ~38.2% → target W5 = W1. |
| 6.6 Std dev | σ ≈ price × vol × √(days/252). Targets within 1–2σ. Fat tails are real. |

## Quiz

```quiz
Q: A leg runs from 200 to 260. Where is the 61.8% retracement?
- 237.1
* 222.9
- 230.0
E: 260 − 0.618 × 60 = 260 − 37.1 = 222.9.
Q: A = 50, B = 70, the pullback C = 62. Where is the 1.618 extension (C + 1.618 × AB)?
- 82.4
* 94.4
- 113.3
E: AB = 20; 1.618 × 20 = 32.4; 62 + 32.4 = 94.4.
Q: In an uptrend count, wave 1 runs 100 → 110 and "wave 2" drops to 98. What do you conclude?
- Wave 2 is just deep
* Rule 1 is broken: this is not wave 2; re-count
- Wave 3 will be stronger
E: Wave 2 can never go beyond the start of wave 1.
Q: Waves measure: wave 1 = 12, wave 3 = 9, wave 5 = 15. Valid?
- Yes
* No: wave 3 is the shortest
- Only if wave 4 is shallow
E: Rule 2: wave 3 is never the shortest of 1, 3, 5.
Q: Wave 1 high is 110. Wave 4 drops to 108.5. What does it mean for the count?
- Normal wave 4
* Rule 3 is broken (overlap with wave 1); relabel unless it's a diagonal
- Wave 5 is guaranteed
E: In an impulse, wave 4 does not enter wave 1's price territory.
Q: A correction where B retraces about 100% of A and C ends near A's end is a…
- Zigzag
* Flat
- Triangle
E: Flat: 3-3-5 with a deep B. Zigzags have a shallow B and a sharp C.
Q: You buy wave 4 at 113.8 in a count where wave 1 topped at 110. Where is the rule-based stop?
* Just below 110 (wave 1's high)
- Just below 100 (wave 1's start)
- At 113.0, close to entry
E: Rule 3 invalidates the count if price trades below wave 1's high.
Q: An index trades at 4,000 with 20% annual volatility. What's the approximate daily 1σ?
- 80 points
* 50 points
- 20 points
E: 4,000 × 0.20 ÷ √252 ≈ 800 ÷ 15.9 ≈ 50.
Q: Why shouldn't you assume a 4σ daily move is "impossible"?
* Market returns have fat tails: extreme moves happen far more often than the normal curve suggests
- Because σ is calculated wrongly
- Because Fibonacci overrides σ
E: Size and stop so that rare, extreme days don't break you.
Q: What are ICT "standard deviation" projections, really?
- Statistical confidence intervals
* Multiples of a reference leg, i.e. a measured-move ruler
- Elliott wave targets
E: They're projections of a leg's length (−1, −2, −2.5…); use them only with confluence.
```

## Self-assessment


> [!check]- Worked problems: Phase 6
> **Q1.** A BOS leg on gold runs from 1,800 to 1,960. Where are the 50% and 61.8% retracements? If the pullback ends at 1,880, where is the 1.618 extension?
> > [!answer]-
> > 50% = **1,880**; 61.8% = 1,960 − 0.618 × 160 = **1,861.1**. Extension = 1,880 + 1.618 × 160 = **2,138.9** (see 6.1).
> **Q2.** Count: wave 1 runs 40 → 52, wave 2 to 45, wave 3 to 63, wave 4 to 54. Is the count valid? Where's the rule-based stop for a wave-4 long, and the W5 = W1 target?
> > [!answer]-
> > Valid: W2 stays above 40, W3 (18) isn't the shortest, W4 (54) stays above W1's high (52). Stop just below **52**; target 54 + 12 = **66** (see 6.3, 6.5).
> **Q3.** A stock at 150 has 30% annual volatility. What's the approximate 1σ move over 5 trading days, and the 2σ range?
> > [!answer]-
> > σ ≈ 150 × 0.30 × √(5 ÷ 252) ≈ **6.3**. 1σ range ≈ 143.7–156.3; 2σ ≈ **137.3–162.7** (see 6.6).

> [!check]- Final check: Phase 6
> **Q1.** State the three Elliott rules and why each one is useful as a stop.
> > [!answer]-
> > W2 never beyond W1's start; W3 never the shortest of 1, 3, 5; W4 never into W1's range (except diagonals). If a rule breaks, the count is wrong, so the trade idea is wrong: that's a natural invalidation level.
> **Q2.** What does a 2σ weekly range not tell you?
> > [!answer]-
> > Direction, and the size of rare moves: returns have fat tails, so moves beyond 2σ happen more often than the normal curve suggests.
